In short

  • Isekai means 'another world' or 'different world.' It is a subgenre of Japanese fantasy and science fiction about a person who is transported to, or reborn in, another world and must survive there.
  • The two main types are tenni, being transported, and tensei, being reincarnated. In reverse isekai, someone from another world lands on Earth.
  • Early works include the novel Warrior from Another World (1979) and the anime Aura Battler Dunbine (1983); the 2012 anime of Sword Art Online set off the modern boom.
  • Isekai made up 14 percent of all new anime releases in 2024.

Isekai is a Japanese fantasy and science fiction genre about a person who is transported to another world and has to survive there. The word means "another world" or "different world." The hero usually arrives by being summoned or by dying and being reborn, often into a medieval fantasy world with magic, monsters and game-like rules, and usually gains powers they never had at home.

What does isekai mean?

Isekai is the Japanese word for "another world," "different world" or "other world." In English it names both the genre and a single work in it. Dictionary.com defines it as a Japanese science fiction or fantasy genre in which the main character is transported to another world, and lists games, animated films and graphic novels as forms it takes.

The genre lives across media. Many isekai start as web novels, become light novels, then manga, then anime. Wikipedia notes that the word itself started to be used around the time of the Sword Art Online boom in the early 2010s, even though stories of this kind are much older.

How do you recognize an isekai story?

Ask two questions: does the hero come from somewhere else, and is the new world strange to them? If both answers are yes, you are probably watching isekai. The hero is usually from modern Japan, often with problems in their old life, and the new world rewards skills or knowledge that were ordinary at home.

Common signs:

  • the hero dies, is summoned or gets pulled into a game in the first episode;
  • the new world is medieval, with magic, limited technology, humans, humanoids and monsters;
  • the hero gets special powers, which fans call "cheats";
  • the story borrows game ideas such as levels, skills and stat screens;
  • the hero ends up at the center of a group of companions.

The most famous cliché is "Truck-kun," the truck that runs the hero over in so many series that fans gave it a name.

What are the types of isekai?

Isekai splits into two main types by how the hero arrives. In tenni, the hero is transported to the other world in their own body. In tensei, the hero dies and is reborn there, keeping memories of the old life. Collider and Wikipedia both use this division. Reverse isekai flips the direction.

Type What happens Examples
Tenni (transported) The hero crosses over alive, often summoned Aura Battler Dunbine
Tensei (reincarnated) The hero dies and is reborn with old memories Mushoku Tensei, That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime
Trapped in a game The hero is stuck inside a virtual world Sword Art Online
Reverse isekai Someone from another world comes to Earth Sally the Witch
Villainess isekai The hero becomes the villainess of an otome game I'm the Villainess, So I'm Taming the Final Boss

Where did isekai come from?

The idea is old. Wikipedia traces it to the Japanese folktale of Urashima Tarō, a fisherman taken to an undersea kingdom who returns home a century later, and to Western books like Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and The Wonderful Wizard of Oz. The modern Japanese genre starts in 1979, and its boom starts in 2012.

Web novel sites, above all Shōsetsuka ni Narō, made the 2010s boom possible: anyone could post a story, and the popular ones were picked up by publishers.

Why is isekai so popular?

Isekai sells escape. Kadokawa editor Satoshi Arima said the stories appeal to office workers who would like to change jobs, because the heroes get to live as they please. The critic Charles Solomon wrote that the genre appeals to people who dream of starting over with a new career and identity.

The audience is older than many people assume. Wikipedia reports a survey in Japan that put the average isekai viewer's age at 46, and a 2024 survey in English-speaking countries that put it at about 30, with 65 percent of viewers male. In 2024 isekai was the second most popular genre in English-speaking territories, just behind action.

Why do some fans dislike isekai?

Mostly because there is so much of it. Collider counted 47 isekai series released in 2021 and 2022 alone, and argues that copied tropes, like the overpowered hero and the harem of admirers, flatten the characters. Even the industry pushed back: writing contests banned isekai entries in 2016 and 2017.

In 2016 a short story contest run by Bungaku Free Market and Shōsetsuka ni Narō banned isekai entries outright, and in 2017 Kadokawa did the same in its own novel contest. Fans also joke about the long titles. Collider's favorite example is Reborn as a Vending Machine, Now I Wander the Dungeon. The length has a practical cause: Narō listed chapters by title only, so writers packed the premise into the title.

Which isekai should you start with?

Pick by the kind of story you want, since isekai mixes with every other genre. Collider's examples cover comedy, drama, action and romance. The well-known series below each show a different side of the genre; most are adapted from novels or manga, so the story often continues on paper.

  1. Sword Art Online (anime 2012), if you want the trapped-in-a-game story that started the modern boom.
  2. Re:Zero (web novel 2012), one of the recent hits Collider names alongside KonoSuba, Overlord and Rising of the Shield Hero.
  3. Mushoku Tensei (web novel 2012), if you want the reincarnation type, where the hero is reborn with his old memories.
  4. The Twelve Kingdoms, the 1990s series that helped popularize the genre before the web novel boom.
  5. Inuyasha (1996), an older classic in which a schoolgirl falls through a well into Japan's past.

Isekai elements are also common in Korean comics; see our guide to manhwa. Keep in mind that isekai describes a plot, while a label like shonen describes the magazine audience, so the two answer different questions. More anime guides are in our anime section.

Questions people ask

What does isekai mean in English?

Isekai is the Japanese word for 'another world' or 'different world.' Dictionary.com defines it as a Japanese science fiction or fantasy genre in which the main character is transported to another world, and also as a single work in that genre, such as a game, animated film or graphic novel.

What was the first isekai?

The genre's roots go back to the folktale of Urashima Tarō and to Alice's Adventures in Wonderland. The first modern Japanese isekai is usually given as Haruka Takachiho's novel Warrior from Another World (1979), and the first isekai anime as Aura Battler Dunbine (1983).

Why do isekai titles get so long?

Many isekai start as web novels on Shōsetsuka ni Narō, a site that listed chapters by title only. J-Novel Club's CEO Samuel Pinarsky said writers responded with descriptive titles to attract readers. Collider's example: Reborn as a Vending Machine, Now I Wander the Dungeon.

Is Inuyasha an isekai?

Collider counts it as an early example. In Inuyasha (1996), Kagome falls down a well and finds herself in Japan's Sengoku period. Some readers would call that time travel, and Wikipedia notes that isekai is usually treated as separate from time-travel stories, so the label is debated.

What is the difference between isekai and portal fantasy?

They share the basic idea: a person from our world crosses into another one. Isekai is the Japanese form, with its own habits: settings borrowed from Japanese role-playing games, reincarnation after death and heroes with 'cheat' powers. Wikipedia lists portal fantasy as a related genre and Oz as one of isekai's roots.
