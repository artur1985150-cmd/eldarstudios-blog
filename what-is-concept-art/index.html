In short

  • Concept art is visual art made to show an idea for a film, game, cartoon, comic or TV show before the final product exists.
  • It designs characters, places, creatures, vehicles and objects, and goes through many versions before one is chosen.
  • Wikipedia says Walt Disney Animation Studios used the term as early as the 1930s.
  • Famous examples: Gustaf Tenggren for Snow White (1937), Mary Blair for Cinderella (1950), Ralph McQuarrie for Star Wars (1977), Syd Mead for Blade Runner (1982).
  • It is not the same as conceptual art, a 1960s art movement about ideas.

Concept art is artwork made to show what a character, place, creature or object in a film, video game, cartoon or comic will look like before it is built. It comes early in production, goes through many versions, and gives the whole team one picture to work toward. Disney used the term as early as the 1930s. Darth Vader and the Spinner cars of Blade Runner both began as concept art.

What does concept art mean?

Wikipedia defines concept art as visual art used to convey an idea for film, video games, animation, comics, TV or other media before it goes into the final product.

Dictionary.com's version is more specific: an artistic rendering of an element of a film, TV show or video game, made during development to visualize characters, costumes, environments and the like.

Put simply, concept art answers "what does this look like?" before anything exists. A concept artist, as Wikipedia puts it, creates a visual design for an item, character or area that does not yet exist.

What does concept art show?

Almost anything that has to be designed. Most of it falls into a few groups, though studios divide the work in their own ways:

  • characters and their costumes
  • environments: cities, rooms, landscapes, whole worlds
  • creatures
  • vehicles, weapons and other props

Wikipedia notes that science fiction and fantasy are the most widely covered areas, which makes sense: the less a world resembles ours, the more of it someone has to invent.

How concept art is made

Concept art is built in rounds. Wikipedia describes several iterations, with multiple solutions explored before a final design is chosen. Artists are expected to produce many versions of one idea and often study real objects for reference.

Early "blue sky" stages are fast and rough: sketches, speed paintings, quick paint-overs on 3D shapes. Later pieces, such as matte paintings, are rendered in much more detail. Along the way the art is used to show progress to directors, clients and investors.

Tools have changed. Earlier concept art was painted in oils and acrylics or drawn with markers and pencils. Today digital painting on a graphics tablet is standard, in programs such as Photoshop, Corel Painter, Clip Studio Paint, Procreate and ArtRage.

Concept art vs storyboard vs mood board vs conceptual art

These four terms get confused because they all come before or around the "real" work. They do different jobs.

Conceptual art is a different world entirely. In it, the idea matters at least as much as how the work looks. Joseph Kosuth's One and Three Chairs (1965), a chair next to its photo and a dictionary definition of "chair", is a classic example.

Where did concept art come from?

According to Wikipedia, Walt Disney Animation Studios used the term "concept art" as early as the 1930s. Wikipedia also notes that concept art was the main medium for film posters from Hollywood's early days.

Gustaf Tenggren is one of the early names. Disney hired the Swedish-born illustrator in 1936, and he worked as a concept artist on Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937), including a presentation drawing of the dwarfs' characters. He later worked on Pinocchio (1940). Outside Disney he illustrated The Poky Little Puppy (1942).

Mary Blair came next. She did concept art and color styling for Cinderella (1950), Alice in Wonderland (1951) and Peter Pan (1953). Walt Disney later asked her to work on It's a Small World, which was built for the 1964 New York World's Fair and later moved to Disneyland. Disney named her a Disney Legend in 1991.

Famous concept artists and the designs they made

Two artists show how far a film design can travel from the first painting.

Ralph McQuarrie (1929–2012) was hired by George Lucas in 1975 to illustrate scenes from the Star Wars script. His paintings helped Lucas persuade 20th Century Fox to fund the film. He designed Darth Vader, combining a samurai helmet with a full-face breathing mask, along with Chewbacca, R2-D2 and C-3PO. He also worked on The Empire Strikes Back (1980) and Return of the Jedi (1983), and won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects for Cocoon (1985).

Syd Mead (1933–2019) trained in industrial design and worked at Ford's Advanced Styling Studio before films. His film work includes Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979), where he designed the alien entity. For Blade Runner (1982) he designed the vehicles, including the Spinners, and then the architecture and street sets. He asked to be credited as a "visual futurist". The same year he designed for Tron, and Katsuhiro Otomo has said Mead's light cycles inspired the motorcycle in Akira. For Aliens (1986) he designed the ship Sulaco.

Where viewers see concept art

Mostly after the fact. Wikipedia notes that once a production is finished, concept art may be reworked and used for advertising, and early film posters were often concept art themselves.

When you do find a studio's concept art, compare it with the finished film. Because each design went through many versions, the differences show what the team tried and dropped, and what survived from the first sketch to the screen. Questions people ask

What is concept art in simple words?

It is the drawing or painting that shows what something in a film or game will look like before anyone builds it. A concept artist might paint a city, a spaceship or a monster so the director, the modelers and the animators all work toward the same picture.

What is the difference between concept art and conceptual art?

Concept art is a design step for films, games and animation. Conceptual art is a movement in fine art from the 1960s, in which the idea behind a work counts at least as much as how it looks. Joseph Kosuth's One and Three Chairs (1965) is a well-known example.

Is concept art the same as a storyboard?

No, though the two are often confused. Concept art designs the world: how a character, place or object looks. A storyboard plans the film shot by shot, in order.

What do concept artists use?

Today mostly digital painting on a graphics tablet, in programs such as Photoshop, Corel Painter, Clip Studio Paint, Procreate and ArtRage. Earlier concept art was done with oil paint, acrylics, markers and pencils, and Wikipedia notes that traditional skills still matter for using the software well.

Is concept art the final design?

Not always. Concept art explores several solutions before one is picked, and the final model, set or animation can change again. Once a project is finished, Wikipedia notes, concept art may be reworked and used in advertising.
