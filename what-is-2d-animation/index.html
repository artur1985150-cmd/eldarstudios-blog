In short

  • 2D animation is created when flat, two-dimensional images are shown in rapid sequence so they seem to move.
  • It covers hand-drawn cel animation, digital drawing and computer-generated vector animation.
  • Cels, transparent sheets for painting characters over a background, were introduced by Earl Hurd and John Bray in 1914.
  • The Rescuers Down Under (1990) was the first major feature to use digital ink and paint entirely; it became the standard.
  • Moving characters are often shot 'on twos': one drawing for every two film frames, 12 drawings per second.

2D animation is animation made from flat, two-dimensional images shown in rapid sequence, so they appear to move. The images can be pencil drawings painted onto clear cels, digital drawings, or vector shapes moved by software. Unlike 3D animation, nothing on screen is a model with real depth: every frame is a flat picture. Traditional hand-drawn animation, the dominant form of the 20th century, is 2D.

What does 2D animation mean?

Dictionary.com defines it as two-dimensional animation, created when two-dimensional images are rapidly sequenced to create the illusion of lifelike motion, as in traditional drawn animation, cel animation or computer-generated vector graphics. The dictionary dates the term to 1985–90.

That is late for a technique far older. Wikipedia calls "traditional animation" a retronym, a new name for an old thing once something else took its place.

What are the types of 2D animation?

The label covers several ways of making the flat images:

Traditional (cel) animation Each frame drawn by hand, inked and painted on transparent cels over a painted background The dominant animation of the 20th century

Computer-assisted (tweened) 2D Artist draws key poses; software fills in the frames between Any production that lets software draw the in-betweens

How was hand-drawn 2D animation made?

As a long chain of departments, each handing drawings to the next. Wikipedia describes the classic process in that order, from script to camera.

First the story becomes a script and a storyboard. Dialogue is generally recorded before animation starts, so movement can be timed to it. Designers draw model sheets, and an animatic puts storyboard and soundtrack together.

Then the animation itself. Key animators draw the key poses, clean-up artists and in-betweeners draw the missing frames, and every drawing is inked onto a cel and painted on the back. Backgrounds were generally painted in gouache or acrylic. Each cel was laid over its background and photographed, one frame at a time.

The cel was invented by Earl Hurd and John Bray in 1914, according to Wikipedia. Its trick is reuse: a character who stands still can stay on one cel while only a talking character is redrawn.

When did 2D animation go digital?

Earlier than most viewers think. Hanna-Barbera was the first American studio to use a computer system for ink and paint. Its lab, led by Marc Levoy from 1980 to 1983, built a system used on roughly a third of the studio's domestic production from 1984 to 1996.

At Disney, the final rainbow shot of The Little Mermaid used digital ink and paint, and The Rescuers Down Under (1990) was the first major feature to use digital ink and paint entirely. All later Disney hand-drawn features followed, using CAPS, a system developed primarily by Pixar. It let artists use colored ink lines and blend hand-drawn characters with 3D backgrounds, as in the ballroom scene of Beauty and the Beast (1991).

So a film that looks hand-painted may never have touched a cel. The drawing stayed by hand; the paint moved to the computer. Wikipedia names Toon Boom Harmony, OpenToonz, RETAS and TVPaint among the software studios use today.

What do "on twos" and limited animation mean?

These are the shortcuts that give 2D its rhythm. Film runs at 24 frames per second, and moving characters are often shot "on twos": one drawing held for two frames, so 12 drawings per second. Quick movements go back to "on ones", because twos are too slow to show them.

Limited animation goes further. In a scene of someone sitting and talking, the body stays the same in every frame and only the head, or just the mouth, is redrawn. Wikipedia says United Productions of America popularized it and Hanna-Barbera used it widely on TV; it is inexpensive to produce, which let cartoons be made on small television budgets.

Even a high-budget studio uses the whole range. According to Wikipedia, Studio Ghibli features mix smooth animation on ones for quick action with animation on threes for dialogue and slow shots, trading some fluidity for complexity in designs and shading. What is the difference between 2D and 3D animation?

A 2D frame is a flat picture; a 3D frame is a view of a digital model. In 2D, artists draw every in-between frame or have software tween flat shapes. In 3D animation, characters are models in a virtual space, and the computer interpolates the frames between key poses.

The border has blurred. Wikipedia's computer animation article lists The Breadwinner (2017) as 2D computer animation praised for its drawn style, and Klaus as a hybrid that uses 3D lighting on 2D animation. From the other side, cel shading makes full 3D look as though it were drawn in a traditional cel style.

How do you recognize 2D animation on screen?

Watch the motion. Holding a drawing for two or three frames gives a slightly stepped rhythm, and in limited animation large parts of a character stay still while the mouth moves.

Look at the picture too: every frame is flat, with depth drawn in rather than modeled. For the craft behind drawn motion, see the 12 principles of animation; more guides are in the cartoons section.

Questions people ask

What is 2D animation in simple words?

It is animation made of flat pictures. Artists draw or build a character on a flat surface, change it slightly from one image to the next, and play the images fast enough that the eye reads them as movement. There is height and width but no real depth: depth is drawn in, not modeled.

What are examples of 2D animation?

Classic Disney features from Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs to The Lion King, Studio Ghibli films, and Hanna-Barbera TV cartoons. Recent films such as The Breadwinner (2017) and Klaus are also 2D; Klaus adds 3D-style lighting to its drawings.

Is 2D animation still hand-drawn?

Often, but rarely on paper all the way. Drawings are scanned into a computer or made digitally, then colored and composited there. Wikipedia calls this digital ink and paint and says it became the standard for animated films and TV.

Is anime 2D or 3D?

Often 2D. Wikipedia uses Studio Ghibli's features as an example of drawn animation that mixes smooth animation on ones with slower animation on threes, trading some fluidity for complexity in designs and shading. 3D can be mixed in with drawn work, and 3D models can be cel-shaded to look drawn.

Is 2D animation easier than 3D?

It is different, not simply easier. In traditional 2D, artists draw the key poses and the in-between frames by hand. In 3D the computer fills in the in-betweens, which Wikipedia calls interpolation.
