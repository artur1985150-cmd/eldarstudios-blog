Shonen is the Japanese label for manga made for teenage boys, and for the anime adapted from that manga. The word 少年 means "young boy." It is a publishing category, not a genre: a series counts as shonen because it ran in a magazine aimed at boys, such as Weekly Shonen Jump. Most shonen stories are about action and fights, but the category also holds sports, comedy and romance, and plenty of adults read it.

What does shonen mean?

Shonen literally means "young boy." In manga it names one of four main reader groups that Japanese publishers sort their magazines by: shonen for boys, shojo for girls, seinen for young men and josei for adult women. Anime picks up the same label from the manga it adapts.

The word used to mean young people in general. Through the 20th century, publishers began to split their magazines by age and sex, and "shonen" narrowed to mean boys' titles. That segmentation sped up from the 1960s and is still how the manga industry files its magazines today. If you are new to the medium itself, our guide to what manga is covers the basics first.

Is shonen a genre?

No. Shonen tells you who the magazine was edited for, not what happens in the story. Inside shonen you find battle series, sports series, school comedies, romance, horror and slice of life. A boxing manga and a detective story can sit side by side in the same weekly issue.

The confusion is understandable. Dictionary.com defines shonen as a subgenre of anime or manga meant to appeal to boys that "tends to be more action-oriented," and that is how most English-speaking fans use the word. Action really does dominate: fights against monsters or other clear forces of evil are the core of the category. But the label itself comes from the magazine shelf.

Who actually reads shonen?

The target reader is a teenage boy, defined in different studies as 10 to 19 or 12 to 21 years old. The real audience is much wider and includes adults and women. A 2006 survey of female manga readers in Japan found that Weekly Shonen Jump was their favorite magazine, ahead of titles made for girls.

You can still see the intended reader in the pages around the comics. Shonen magazines carry ads and features chosen for young males, such as video games, often tied to the series in that issue: a game adaptation, or a film of the magazine's biggest hit.

How do you tell if a manga or anime is shonen?

Look up the magazine the manga was first serialized in. If that magazine is aimed at boys, the series is shonen, and so is its anime. Tone, violence and art style are unreliable clues. Here is a quick check that works for almost any title:

  1. Find the original manga. Most shonen anime are adaptations; an anime-original show has no magazine and is labeled by the studio and marketing instead.
  2. Read the "magazine" line on the manga's publisher page or encyclopedia entry.
  3. Match the magazine against the list below. "Shonen" in the title is a strong hint, but check the publisher's own description if you can.
  4. If the series moved magazines, note both. The category can change mid-run.

Which magazines publish shonen manga?

The big weekly shonen magazines are Shueisha's Weekly Shonen Jump, Kodansha's Weekly Shonen Magazine and Shogakukan's Weekly Shonen Sunday. There are monthlies and apps too. The official Jump site describes it as Shueisha's shonen manga magazine, and its current lineup includes One Piece, Sakamoto Days and Kagurabachi.

Weekly Shonen Magazine and Shonen Sunday, both launched in 1959, were the first weekly shonen magazines. Jump arrived in 1968 and became the best-selling manga magazine of any category, a position it still holds.

Frieren is a good reminder that shonen is not only fights. It follows an elf mage who returns fifty years after her party's quest and finds her human friends grown old. It runs in a boys' weekly and won the 2024 Kodansha Manga Award in the shonen category.

Dark series that are still shonen

A grim story does not move a series into seinen. Attack on Titan, where humanity hides behind walls from giant man-eating Titans, ran in Bessatsu Shonen Magazine from start to finish. Death Note, about a high school student whose notebook kills anyone whose name he writes in it, ran in Weekly Shonen Jump. Both are shonen.

Chainsaw Man is another one. Tatsuki Fujimoto's series ran in Weekly Shonen Jump from 2018 to 2020, then its second part moved to the Shonen Jump+ app from 2022 to 2026. Different format, same category.

The opposite move happens too. Vinland Saga began in Weekly Shonen Magazine in April 2005, then moved to Monthly Afternoon that December and finished there in 2025. Kodansha's own site calls Afternoon a seinen manga magazine, so most of Vinland Saga is seinen. Our page on what seinen means covers that side of the shelf.

Shonen vs shojo, seinen and josei

All four labels work the same way: they name the reader a magazine is edited for. Shonen is the most popular of the four in Japan. The table shows the split, though in practice every category is read well outside its target group.

Since the 1980s, shonen and shojo have borrowed from each other. The large expressive eyes associated with shojo became common in shonen, and so did shojo panel montages. Female characters also got bigger roles, and more women began to write and draw shonen series. For the wider picture of how these categories carry over to screen, see what anime is or browse our anime section.

Where shonen came from

Japan's boys' magazines are older than manga as we know it. Shonen Sekai, the first youth magazine, launched in 1895 for boys and girls alike. Girls' magazines split off in 1902, and boys' titles followed. Shonen Pakku, from 1907, was the first boys' magazine to print manga, and Shonen Club followed in 1914.

After the war, Osamu Tezuka's long, cinematic "story manga" such as Astro Boy changed what boys' comics could be. The 1959 weeklies and the 1968 launch of Jump built the modern market. In the 1980s, battle manga took over with Fist of the North Star and Dragon Ball, and critic Jason Thompson credits Dragon Ball with the cartoonish art style many shonen hits still use. The same magazine check works for newer labels too, from isekai fantasy to chibi-style comedy: the story type and the reader category are two different questions.

Questions people ask

What does shonen mean in anime?

It means the anime is based on, or made in the style of, manga aimed at teenage boys. The Japanese word 少年 means 'young boy'. Most shonen anime are adaptations of series from shonen magazines, so the label really describes the target reader of the original magazine, not the content of the show.

Is shonen a genre?

No. Shonen is a demographic category used by Japanese publishers. Inside it you find many genres: battle stories, sports, comedy, romance, horror and slice of life. Dictionary.com calls it a subgenre of anime or manga aimed at boys, which is how many English speakers use the word, but publishers sort by magazine, not by plot.

Is Attack on Titan shonen or seinen?

Shonen. Hajime Isayama's manga ran in Kodansha's Bessatsu Shonen Magazine from 2009 to 2021. Its violence makes many viewers guess seinen, but the category comes from the magazine, and Bessatsu Shonen Magazine is a shonen title.

What is the most famous shonen magazine?

Weekly Shonen Jump, published by Shueisha since 1968. It is the best-selling manga magazine across all categories, and it published Dragon Ball, Slam Dunk, Naruto, Bleach and One Piece. Shueisha's English site singles Jump out as very popular in Japan and overseas.

Can girls and adults read shonen?

Yes, and they do. The target reader is a boy of roughly 10 to 19 or 12 to 21, depending on the study, but the real readership covers all ages and genders. A 2006 survey of female manga readers found that Weekly Shonen Jump was their favorite magazine.
