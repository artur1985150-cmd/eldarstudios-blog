It covers TV series, films and web shows in every genre, and it is often adapted from manga, Japan's comics.

What does anime mean in Japan and in English?

The same word means two different things depending on who says it. In Japanese, anime is simply the everyday word for animation, so a Disney film is anime too. In English, the word narrowed to animation produced in Japan. Cambridge Dictionary defines it as Japanese films made with drawn characters and images.

Dictionary definitions also split on style versus origin. Dictionary.com describes anime as animation "created in or influenced by" the Japanese 2D style, which lets in Western shows that copy the look. Other definitions require the work to be made in Japan. Before anime caught on, English speakers used "Japanimation" through the 1970s and 1980s; anime began replacing it in the mid-1980s.

What is anime short for?

Anime is short for animēshon, the Japanese loan of the English word animation, cut down to its first three syllables. Some people say the word comes from the French dessin animé, "animated drawing." Wikipedia notes that others call this a myth born from anime's popularity in France in the late 1970s and 1980s.

In English it is pronounced roughly "AN-ih-may." It usually works as a mass noun, like "music": you ask "how much anime have you watched?", not "how many animes." Dictionary.com dates its first English use to 1985-90.

How do you recognize anime?

Look at the eyes, the movement and the camera. Many anime characters have large, expressive eyes, a habit traced to Osamu Tezuka, who was influenced by Disney characters and Betty Boop. Motion is often limited: fewer drawn frames, more held poses, with the effort going into detailed backgrounds and camera-style shots such as pans, zooms and angles.

None of these is a rule. Hayao Miyazaki's films use realistically sized eyes and natural hair colors. Most anime characters are drawn five to eight heads tall, but the same show may shrink them into tiny, big-headed versions for a joke. That squashed style has its own name, chibi.

Other common signs:

  • a shorthand of symbols for feelings, like a sweat drop for nervousness or a throbbing vein for anger;
  • hair in bright, unnatural colors that moves with the action;
  • an opening song, often J-pop or J-rock, written for the series;
  • stories that run across many episodes instead of resetting each week.

A short history of anime in dates

Japanese animation is more than a century old. The first professional, publicly shown works appeared in 1917, and the modern TV look arrived in the 1960s with Osamu Tezuka. These are the dates that matter most to a viewer:

Year What happened
1917 First commercial Japanese animated films; Jun'ichi Kōuchi's Namakura Gatana is the oldest that survives
1945 Momotaro: Sacred Sailors, the first feature-length anime film
1963 Astro Boy begins on 1 January, directed by Tezuka from his own manga
1969 Sazae-san starts; it becomes the longest-running animated TV series
1988 Akira, one of the high-budget films of Japan's bubble years
1995 Neon Genesis Evangelion and Ghost in the Shell
2003 Spirited Away wins the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature

Tezuka's official site calls Astro Boy the first Japanese 30-minute animated series, with a top viewer rating of 40.7 percent. To work on a tight TV schedule, Tezuka simplified Disney techniques and cut frame counts. Those shortcuts became part of how anime looks.

Is anime a genre?

No. Anime is a medium, the way film or comics is a medium, and it holds almost every genre: science fiction, fantasy, romance, sports, horror, comedy and mecha, the giant robot stories that took shape around Tezuka and grew with Gundam. Wikipedia's anime article flags "anime is a genre" as a common misclassification.

What confuses people is that anime is also sorted by audience, and those labels sound like genres. Shonen means the source manga ran in a magazine for boys; seinen means a magazine for young men. These labels come from the publisher, not from the plot. A shonen series can be a sports drama, a fantasy war story or a romantic comedy.

Some genres do grow inside anime. Isekai, stories where a character is transported to another world, is one of the most common on today's schedules. For a wider look at where anime sits among other techniques, see our guide to types of animation.

Where does anime come from: manga, novels, games?

Much anime starts as something else. Many series adapt manga; since the 2000s, more and more adapt light novels and visual novels, such as The Melancholy of Haruhi Suzumiya and Fate/stay night, both from 2006. Video games are another source. Original stories exist too, especially in films.

This matters for a viewer because the source often continues past the anime. If a series ends on a cliffhanger, the manga or novel it came from may already have the rest of the story.

Which anime should you watch first?

Start with one well-known film and one well-known series, then follow what you liked. A film shows the medium at its most polished in two hours; a series shows how anime tells long stories. Pick by genre you already enjoy elsewhere, not by what is trending.

  1. Spirited Away (2001), Hayao Miyazaki's Studio Ghibli film. It won the Oscar for Best Animated Feature and suits any age.
  2. Astro Boy (1963), if you want to see where TV anime began. It is black and white and made for children.
  3. Cowboy Bebop (1998) or Ghost in the Shell (1995), for adult science fiction that found an international audience in the 1990s.
  4. A recent shonen hit such as Demon Slayer or Jujutsu Kaisen, both among the ten most discussed TV shows on Twitter worldwide in 2021.
  5. An isekai series, if you like fantasy and games.

Subtitles or dubbing is a matter of taste. Anime has been dubbed and subtitled for foreign audiences since the 1980s, and Dragon Ball Z and Sailor Moon were dubbed into more than a dozen languages. More guides on series, films and terms are in our anime section.

Questions people ask

What is anime short for?

It is a shortening of animēshon, the Japanese spelling of the English word animation. Some writers have claimed it comes from the French dessin animé, but that origin is disputed. Dictionary.com dates the English use of anime to 1985-90.

Is anime a genre?

No. Anime is a medium, like film or comics, and it holds every genre: science fiction, romance, sports, horror, comedy and more. Calling it a genre is a common mistake that Wikipedia's anime article points out directly.

What is the difference between anime and cartoons?

In Japan there is none: anime is the word for all animation. In English, anime means animation made in Japan. It tends toward limited animation, detailed backgrounds, camera-like shots and continuing stories across episodes, while many older Western cartoons were episodic.

What was the first anime?

Public, professional Japanese animation began in 1917. The oldest surviving film from that year is Jun'ichi Kōuchi's Namakura Gatana. Astro Boy, from 1963, is often treated as the start of modern TV anime.

Are anime and manga the same thing?

No. Manga are Japanese comics on paper or screen; anime is animation. Many anime are adaptations of manga, and some go the other way, but they are different media with different creators.
