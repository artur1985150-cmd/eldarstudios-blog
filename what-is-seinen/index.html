In short

  • Seinen (青年, 'youth') is the publishing label for manga aimed at young adult men, roughly 18 to 30, and for anime adapted from it.
  • A series is seinen because it ran in a seinen magazine such as Weekly Young Jump, Morning, Afternoon, Big Comic or Young Magazine.

Seinen is the Japanese label for manga made for young adult men, and for anime adapted from it. The word 青年 means "youth," but in publishing it marks magazines edited for men of roughly 18 to 30. A series is seinen because it ran in one of those magazines, such as Weekly Young Jump, Morning or Big Comic. Seinen stories cover the same genres as boys' manga but often go darker and slower.

What does seinen mean?

Seinen means "youth" in Japanese. As a manga term it names one of the four main reader groups that publishers sort magazines by, next to shonen (boys), shojo (girls) and josei (adult women). The target reader is a man aged about 18 to 30, and some definitions stretch it to 40.

Older men read seinen too; the label simply gets used less the older the intended reader becomes. Anime inherits the word from its source, so a "seinen anime" almost always means an adaptation of a manga that ran in a seinen magazine. For the basics of the medium, start with what manga is.

Is seinen a genre?

No. Seinen describes who a magazine is edited for, not what its stories are about. Seinen magazines run action, war, romance, slice of life, comedy and crime, the same genres you find in shonen. What changes is the treatment: plots tend to be more mature, and sex and violence can be shown more directly.

That is why a seinen shelf can hold a sword epic, a cooking comedy and a medical drama at once. Fans sometimes say "seinen" to mean "dark and serious," but that is a shortcut, and as you will see below, it often points the wrong way.

How is seinen different from shonen?

The difference is the reader each magazine is built for. Shonen magazines aim at teenage boys, seinen magazines at young working men. In practice, seinen leans toward older protagonists, slower plots and harder themes, while shonen leans toward teen heroes and clear goals. Both are read far beyond their targets.

The same publishers run both. Shueisha, the house behind Weekly Shonen Jump, entered the seinen market in 1979 with Weekly Young Jump, whose official site calls it Shueisha's seinen comic magazine. Our guide to what shonen means covers the other side in detail.

Is seinen the same as adult manga?

No. Japan's Publishing Science Research Institute, which has tracked manga sales since 1979, counts seinen magazines separately from a smaller category of "mature" magazines. That mature group holds erotic manga and other censored material, usually from small specialist presses. Seinen means mainstream titles for men from big houses like Shueisha and Kodansha.

The word choice has a history. Publishers marketed general adult content as "youth" manga partly to avoid the stigma and official scrutiny that came with an explicit "adult" label. So seinen is closer to "manga for grown-ups" than to "manga for adults only."

How do you tell if a manga or anime is seinen?

Find the magazine the manga first ran in. If that magazine is a seinen title, the series is seinen, and so is its anime. Publishers say this openly: Kodansha's sites for Morning and Afternoon each describe the magazine as Kodansha's seinen manga magazine. A quick check:

  1. Look up the original manga, not the anime.
  2. Find the "serialized in" or "magazine" line on the publisher's page or an encyclopedia entry.
  3. Check the magazine's own site or description for the word seinen (青年).
  4. If the series switched magazines, note when. The label can change mid-run.

Series people mistake for seinen

Dark does not mean seinen. Attack on Titan ran in Kodansha's Bessatsu Shonen Magazine from 2009 to 2021. Death Note ran in Weekly Shonen Jump from 2003 to 2006. Chainsaw Man ran in Weekly Shonen Jump and then the Shonen Jump+ app. All three are shonen, however grim they get.

Vinland Saga shows the reverse. Makoto Yukimura started it in Weekly Shonen Magazine in April 2005, then moved it to Monthly Afternoon that December, where it ran until July 2025. Since Afternoon is a seinen magazine, almost the whole series is seinen, and its first few months were shonen.

Where seinen came from

Seinen took shape in the late 1960s, when Japan's postwar baby boomers grew up and kept reading manga. Realistic "gekiga" comics from the rental-book market showed there was an adult audience, and big publishers launched magazines for it. Some scholars date the start to Hōbunsha's Comic Magazine in May 1966.

Futabasha's Weekly Manga Action followed in 1967, with Monkey Punch's Lupin III in its first issue. Big Comic arrived in 1968 and pitched seinen as something between pop entertainment and literature. A second wave came with Weekly Young Jump (1979), Big Comic Spirits (1980) and Kodansha's Morning (1982), and by the 1990s seinen made up about a third of all manga published. Women joined too: Rumiko Takahashi and Fumi Saimon were among the artists working for seinen magazines in the 1980s. Questions people ask

What is seinen in simple words?

Seinen is manga written for young men rather than teenage boys, plus the anime based on it. The word means 'youth'. Publishers use it to label magazines like Weekly Young Jump and Morning, so a seinen series is one that was serialized in one of those magazines.

What is the difference between seinen and shonen?

The reader they are edited for. Shonen magazines aim at boys of about 10 to 21, seinen magazines at men of about 18 to 30 or older. Both share genres like action, war, romance and comedy, but seinen tends to have darker stories and can show sex and violence more directly.

Is seinen the same as adult or explicit manga?

No. Japan's Publishing Science Research Institute counts seinen magazines separately from a smaller 'mature' category that holds erotic and other censored material. Seinen means mainstream titles for men from big publishers such as Shueisha and Kodansha. Many seinen series contain nothing explicit at all.

Is Attack on Titan seinen?

No, it is shonen. Hajime Isayama's manga ran in Kodansha's Bessatsu Shonen Magazine from 2009 to 2021. Death Note and Chainsaw Man are in the same situation: grim stories, shonen magazines.

What are good seinen anime to start with?

Start with series whose manga ran in a seinen magazine and that have well-known anime: Monster (Big Comic Original), Mushishi (Afternoon), Vinland Saga (mostly Afternoon), and Kingdom, Golden Kamuy, Tokyo Ghoul or Oshi no Ko (all Weekly Young Jump). Akira (Young Magazine) is the classic from the 1980s.
