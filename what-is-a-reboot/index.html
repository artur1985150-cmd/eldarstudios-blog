In short

  • In film and TV, a reboot is a new start for an existing series: the story begins again and earlier continuity is dropped.
  • A remake retells one earlier work; a reboot restarts a whole series, usually so more instalments can follow.
  • A sequel continues the story, a prequel goes back before it, and a spin-off follows other characters or another part of the same world.
  • The word comes from computing; Wikipedia traces its first known use for an entertainment franchise to a 1994 Usenet post.
  • Batman Begins (2005) rebooted the Batman films and helped make reboot a common Hollywood term.

In film and television, a reboot is a new start for an established series. The story begins again from the beginning, usually with new actors in the old roles, and the events of the earlier films or episodes stop counting. Batman Begins (2005) is the textbook case: it restarted the Batman films from scratch after Warner Bros. cancelled further films following Batman & Robin (1997). (In computing, to reboot simply means to restart a machine; this page is about stories.)

What does reboot mean in movies and TV?

Wikipedia defines a reboot as "a new start to an established fictional universe, work, or series." Reboots usually drop continuity and rebuild the characters, plot and backstory from the start.

The commercial logic is plain. Mental Floss says a reboot implies a franchise is being reworked and updated in the hope of producing more instalments. A reboot is usually a bet on a series, not on one film.

Where does the word reboot come from?

From computers. Wikipedia notes that the meaning changed on the way: rebooting a computer restarts the same program unaltered, while a story reboot revises the narrative from the beginning.

The first known use of the word for an entertainment franchise was in a 1994 Usenet posting. Wikipedia's article on Batman Begins says the film "reboots the Batman film series" and helped popularise the term reboot in Hollywood. Warner Bros. had cancelled further Batman films after Batman & Robin (1997) was panned by critics and underperformed at the box office; Christopher Nolan's film started the story over.

Reboot vs remake vs sequel vs prequel vs spin-off

These five words describe five different relationships to an earlier work. We put them in one table, with examples taken from the sources listed at the end of this page.

Two quick rules follow from the table. If the old events still happened, it is not a reboot. If only one film is being redone, it is a remake.

How is a reboot different from a remake?

A remake retells the story of one earlier production in the same medium, usually with a different cast. Wikipedia's examples include Ocean's Eleven (2001), a remake of Ocean's 11 (1960), and Scarface (1983), which turned the alcohol traffickers of the 1932 film into cocaine smugglers.

A reboot works on a series. ScreenCrush puts the difference in one line: a remake is a new version of one film, a reboot a new version of a multipart film series. By that test Friday the 13th (2009) is a reboot, because the series it restarted began with a film in 1980. Casino Royale (2006) is another: it reset James Bond's continuity.

The line is not always clean. ScreenCrush argues that RoboCop (2014), made after the 1987 original, fits either label. By ScreenCrush's definition Peter Jackson's King Kong is a reboot, yet it finds it ludicrous not to call the film a remake.

What is a soft reboot?

A soft reboot keeps some continuity with the original series but changes the style, tone or intent, according to Wikipedia.

Wikipedia's examples include Halloween H20: 20 Years Later (1998), Halloween (2018) and Terminator: Dark Fate (2019). Star Trek (2009) and Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) are listed as debated cases, somewhere between soft reboot and sequel. The makers of Fury Road, Mental Floss notes, preferred the word "revisit".

A related idea is the legacy sequel. Wikipedia's article on sequels describes it as a film that follows the continuity of the original but takes place much further along the timeline. The film journalist Pamela McClintock, quoted there, says such films exploit goodwill toward the past while launching a new generation of actors and stories.

Sequels, prequels and spin-offs: the ones that keep the story

All three keep the earlier events intact. What changes is the direction.

A sequel continues the story of, or expands upon, an earlier work, in Wikipedia's words. Wikipedia notes that Francis Ford Coppola claims to have popularised numbered sequel titles with The Godfather Part II (1974).

A prequel tells events that happen before the original. The word is a 20th-century coinage; the Oxford English Dictionary and Merriam-Webster date its first print appearance to 1922, in Publishers' Weekly. Release order and story order can differ. Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom (1984) is set in 1935, a year before the events of Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981).

A spin-off is derived from an existing work but focuses on different aspects of it. One of the earliest, Wikipedia says, was the radio comedy The Great Gildersleeve (1941–1957), spun off from Fibber McGee and Molly. Labels can overlap: Better Call Saul is a spin-off of Breaking Bad and, according to its Wikipedia article, mainly a prequel to it.

There are also in-between forms. A midquel takes place between the events of earlier works; Rogue One (2016) is one of Wikipedia's examples.

Reboot or revival? The TV version of the question

Television adds one more word. A revival is a series that returns with new episodes after being off the air for some time. Wikipedia's article on revivals says they bring back most or many of the original storylines, characters and locations, often to finish story arcs left open.

Reboots and remakes, by contrast, are usually played by new cast members and ignore the events of the original series. So if the original actors return and the old story continues, you are watching a revival, whatever the marketing says. If new actors play the same characters from scratch, it is a reboot.

The word reboot has stretched in recent years. Wikipedia notes that in the late 2010s it was used as a catch-all for sequel series and remakes in general, which is one reason it is criticised as a vague buzzword.

How to tell what you are watching

When a new film or series arrives with a familiar title, four questions sort it out:

  1. Did the events of the earlier films or seasons still happen? If not, it is a reboot or a remake.
  2. Is it redoing one specific film or episode, or a whole series? One film points to a remake, a series to a reboot.
  3. Does the story move forward, backward or sideways? That separates a sequel, a prequel and a spin-off.
  4. Are the original actors back, continuing the old story? On TV, that is a revival.

Series built from short self-contained parts handle this differently. An anthology series can change its cast every season without anyone calling it a reboot, and a limited series usually ends before the question comes up. New series start with a pilot episode, and many online shows begin as a web series. Sequels and new seasons often open by resolving a cliffhanger. Questions people ask

What is a reboot in simple words?

A reboot starts a film or TV series over. New actors usually play the old characters, the origin story is told again, and the events of the earlier films or episodes no longer count. Batman Begins (2005), which restarted the Batman films after Batman & Robin (1997), is the classic example.

What is the difference between a reboot and a remake?

A remake is a new version of one film or show, like Psycho (1998), which redid Psycho (1960) almost shot for shot. A reboot restarts a whole series. ScreenCrush sums it up as one film versus a multipart series, which is why Friday the 13th (2009) counts as a reboot of a series that began in 1980.

Is a reboot the same as a sequel?

No. A sequel continues an earlier story, while a reboot throws that story away and starts again. Some films sit in between: a soft reboot keeps part of the old continuity but changes the style or tone, and critics still argue whether Star Trek (2009) and Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) are reboots or sequels.

What is a soft reboot?

Wikipedia defines it as a reboot that shares some continuity with the original series but changes the style, tone or intent. Halloween (2018) and Terminator: Dark Fate (2019) are listed as examples.

Why is it called a reboot?

The word was borrowed from computing, where rebooting means restarting a machine. The meaning shifted on the way: a computer restarts the same program unchanged, while a film reboot rewrites the story from the beginning.
