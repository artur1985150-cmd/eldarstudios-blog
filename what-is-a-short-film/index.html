In short

  • A short film is a complete film with a short running time; the Academy Awards set the limit at 40 minutes including credits.
  • Other bodies draw the line elsewhere: Sundance at 49 minutes, the Cannes short film competition at 15.
  • The first films ever shown, in the 1890s, were shorts; features took over between about 1910 and 1915.
  • Shorts come in the same kinds as features: narrative, documentary, animated and experimental.
  • Many directors start with a short, and some shorts become features, like Whiplash.

A short film is a complete, standalone film with a running time much shorter than a feature. The most quoted definition comes from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, which counts as a short "an original motion picture that has a running time of not more than 40 minutes including all credits". Other festivals and academies draw the line in different places. Shorts can be live action, animated or documentary, usually seen at festivals or online.

What counts as a short film?

A short film tells its whole story, or makes its whole point, in a short running time. Indie Shorts Mag puts it in one line: a standalone motion picture that is shorter than a feature film.

"Standalone" matters. The Academy's rules, as Indie Shorts Mag summarises them, leave out trailers, adverts, sequences cut from feature films and works made as episodes of a TV series. A short is not a piece of something longer. It is the whole thing.

In the United States shorts were long called "short subjects", when a film fit on two reels or fewer. Wikipedia notes that "short" began as an abbreviation of that term.

How long is a short film? Limits compared

There is no world standard. Each organisation that gives prizes to shorts writes its own limit:

Academy Awards (Oscars) 40 minutes, including all credits
Academy of Canadian Cinema and Television 59 minutes for scripted films, 45 for documentaries
Sundance Film Festival 49 minutes or under, including credits
Festival de Cannes, short film competition up to 15 minutes, including credits
Cannes Short Film Corner 1 to 35 minutes

The figures come from Wikipedia (Oscars, Canada) and Indie Shorts Mag (Sundance, Cannes). They are rules for entering competitions, and they change, so a filmmaker should always check the current year's rules.

For a viewer, the practical point is that "short" has no fixed length: it depends on who is asking.

Where did short films come from?

Films at the start of cinema were very short, sometimes a minute or less. The first short films were shown in 1891 on Thomas Edison's kinetoscope, which one person could watch at a time.

Early films were often a single shot of everyday life or a royal procession. The Lumière brothers' Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat (1895) is one of the best known.

These films copied the entertainment of the time: music halls, fairground acts and magic lantern shows, whose programmes lasted up to about 20 minutes. Georges Méliès's A Trip to the Moon (1902) and Edwin S. Porter's The Great Train Robbery (1903) belong to this period.

Longer films arrived in the 1910s. Between about 1910 and 1915 the average film grew from 18 to 75 minutes, Wikipedia says, thanks to better cameras, competition between studios and audience demand.

The golden age of shorts and the cinema programme

Shorts did not disappear when features arrived. They moved into the cinema programme. A 1930s show could include a feature film plus newsreels, an animated cartoon, a two-reel comedy and a chapter of a serial. Wikipedia counts the 1920s to the 1950s as the "golden age" of short films.

Big studios had whole departments for comedies, cartoons and newsreels. Hal Roach's Our Gang series and Charlie Chaplin's Tramp were among the great successes of the silent era. Silent serials ran up to 20 chapters and sound-era ones 12 to 15. Each chapter ended on a suspenseful note, and viewers were expected to come back the next week. The cliffhanger comes from there.

Television ended most of this. Live-action studio shorts were nearly dead by 1956, and The Three Stooges released their last two-reel comedy in 1959. Cartoons lasted longer, partly because they could be rerun on television. The Pink Panther, which ran from 1964 to 1980, was the last regular theatrical cartoon series.

Types of short films

Shorts come in the same kinds as features. Indie Shorts Mag groups them like this, with examples from its own list:

  • Narrative shorts tell fictional stories. Jim Cummings's Thunder Road (2016, 13 minutes) won the Short Film Grand Jury Prize at Sundance.
  • Documentary shorts look at real people and events. Alain Resnais's Night and Fog (1956, 32 minutes) is about the Holocaust and the remains of Nazi camps.
  • Animated shorts use drawing, computer animation or stop motion. Matthew A. Cherry's Hair Love (2019, 7 minutes) won the Academy Award for Animated Short Film, and Pixar's Bao (2018) was directed by Domee Shi.
  • Experimental shorts care more about image, sound and rhythm than plot. Luis Buñuel and Salvador Dalí's Un Chien Andalou (1929, 16 minutes) is the classic surrealist example.

Animation has a particularly long history in short form. Disney, Warner Bros., MGM and Walter Lantz all made cartoon shorts for cinemas; Tom and Jerry continued at MGM until 1967. If you want to know how those cartoons were made, see types of animation.

Shorts that became features

A short is often a calling card. Wikipedia describes shorts as a way to gain experience and to show talent to investors and studios, and they are usually paid for with grants, sponsors or the filmmakers' own money.

Indie Shorts Mag points to two cases where it worked:

  • Damien Chazelle's Whiplash (2013, about 18 minutes) was a short before it became the basis for his feature film.
  • David F. Sandberg's Lights Out, about three minutes long, led to a feature adaptation.

Where to watch short films today

Festivals are the main home of shorts. Wikipedia names three of the oldest dedicated festivals: Oberhausen in Germany (since 1954), Tampere in Finland (since 1969) and Clermont-Ferrand in France (since 1979). Big general festivals such as Sundance, Cannes, Berlin and Toronto also screen shorts.

Online, YouTube and Vimeo have large communities of short filmmakers, and curated sites such as Short of the Week and Omeleto pick the best. ShortsTV is a television channel for shorts, and every February and March the Oscar-nominated shorts get a cinema release in the US, UK, Canada and Mexico.

You also meet shorts without looking for them. Pixar has made over three dozen since André & Wally B. in 1984, and pairs them with its features in cinemas. DreamWorks often makes a short sequel for the special edition home video releases of its major films. A web series is a close cousin, but its episodes belong to a longer run.

Short film vs feature film

The difference is not only length. A short usually follows one or a few characters in a single situation, Wikipedia notes; a reel or two left no room for more. A feature has space for subplots and a large cast.

Indie Shorts Mag says short filmmakers often have to communicate more with less, so editing and sound do a lot of the work. See montage and Foley for two tools of that craft. For more on how films are built, browse our film section.

Questions people ask

How long is a short film?

There is no single rule. For the Academy Awards a short film runs 40 minutes or less, including all credits. The Academy of Canadian Cinema and Television allows up to 59 minutes for scripted films, Sundance takes shorts of 49 minutes or under, and the Cannes short film competition caps them at 15 minutes.

Is a 60-minute film a short film?

By the Oscars' rule, no: anything over 40 minutes counts as a feature there. Some organisations are more generous; the Canadian Academy accepts scripted shorts of up to 59 minutes. A film of exactly an hour is past every limit in the table of this article.

What is the minimum length of a short film?

Most definitions set only a maximum. Indie Shorts Mag notes there is no minimum runtime, and some celebrated shorts are tiny: PES's Fresh Guacamole lasts 1 minute 40 seconds and was nominated for the Academy Award for Animated Short Film. The first films of the 1890s sometimes ran a minute or less.

Where can I watch short films?

At festivals, which are the main stage for shorts, and online. Wikipedia names YouTube and Vimeo, plus curated sites such as Short of the Week and Omeleto. ShortsTV is a channel devoted to shorts, and each February and March the Oscar-nominated shorts are released in cinemas in the US, UK, Canada and Mexico.

Why do Pixar films have a short before them?

Shorts were once a normal part of the cinema programme, when a feature came with newsreels, cartoons and comedies. Pixar has made over three dozen animated shorts since André & Wally B. in 1984 and pairs shorts with its features in cinemas.
