In short

  • Abstract art is painting, sculpture or graphic art in which things from the visible world play little or no part; it works through shape, color, line, tone and texture.
  • There are two kinds: art simplified from a real object until it is hard to recognize, and pure abstraction with no source in the visible world at all.
  • Kandinsky was long called the first abstract painter (1910–11), but Hilma af Klint painted her first abstract work in 1906.
  • After the Second World War, American Abstract Expressionism (Pollock, Rothko, de Kooning) made abstraction a mainstream approach.
  • Abstract art moves on screen too: Disney's Fantasia opens with abstract animation, and Norman McLaren painted straight onto film.

Abstract art is art that does not try to show anything from the visible world. Instead of a face, a tree or a room, it uses shapes, colors, lines and brush marks to make its effect. Some abstract works start from a real object and simplify it until it is hard to recognize; others are pure abstraction with no real source at all. It took shape in Europe in the years just before the First World War.

What is abstract art in simple words?

Abstract art is a picture or sculpture with nothing in it to recognize. Britannica defines it as painting, sculpture or graphic art in which things from the visible world play little or no part. What is left is color, shape, line, tone and texture, and those become the subject.

Every picture has these elements. A portrait is also patches of color and a few lines. Before the 20th century, artists used them to describe people, places and stories. Abstract artists kept the elements and dropped the description.

Painter Maurice Denis put the idea early, in 1890: before it is a war horse, a nude or a story, a picture is a flat surface covered with colors in a certain order. Abstract art takes that sentence literally.

What are the two kinds of abstract art?

Tate separates two kinds. One starts from a real object, figure or landscape and simplifies it into shapes. The other uses geometric shapes or gestural marks that have no source in the visible world at all. Artists of the second kind sometimes called it "concrete" or "non-objective" art.

In practice everyone says "abstract" for both, and Tate admits the line between them is not always obvious. A useful test for a viewer: can you guess where the shapes came from? If a blur of verticals still reads as trees, it is abstracted from nature. If the canvas is just a grid of colored squares, it is pure abstraction.

Who made the first abstract painting?

For most of the 20th century the answer was Wassily Kandinsky, who was regarded as the first modern artist to paint purely abstract pictures, in 1910–11. Britannica notes that the story was questioned in the 21st century: the Swedish artist Hilma af Klint painted her first abstract work in 1906, though with a different goal.

The move did not belong to one person. In the four or five years before the First World War, Robert Delaunay, Kandinsky, Kazimir Malevich and Vladimir Tatlin all turned to fundamentally abstract art. Tate names Malevich and Piet Mondrian as the pioneers of pure abstract painting from about 1910 to 1920, and the Russian constructivist Naum Gabo as a pioneer of abstract sculpture.

The ground had been prepared. Britannica lists Fauvism, Expressionism, Cubism and Futurism as movements that had already widened the gap between art and natural appearances. Cubist and fauvist painters still painted real things, but they bent them far enough to open the door.

How did abstract art develop after that?

It grew in bursts. During the First World War, the de Stijl group in the Netherlands and Dada in Zurich widened abstract art further. Between the wars it got little attention, as Surrealism and socially critical Realism brought images back. After 1945, American Abstract Expressionism made it a mainstream approach.

Abstract Expressionism is the branch most people picture when they hear the words. Tate describes it as new forms of abstract art developed by American painters such as Jackson Pollock, Mark Rothko and Willem de Kooning in the 1940s and 1950s, often with gestural brushstrokes and an impression of spontaneity. Most of them worked in New York, which is why they were also called the New York school.

From the 1950s, Britannica says, abstract art was an accepted and widely practiced approach in European and American painting and sculpture. In the 1960s minimalism pushed it to an extreme: Tate describes artworks made of simple geometric shapes based on the square and the rectangle, with no attempt to represent anything outside them. How do you look at an abstract painting?

Look at it the way you listen to music without lyrics. Stop searching for a hidden object and ask what the colors, shapes and marks do to you, and how they are arranged. Four plain steps help.

  1. Look from far away first. Notice the overall balance: is it calm and even, or crowded on one side?
  2. Walk closer and look at the surface. Are the marks fast and loose, or slow and precise? That tells you a lot about how it was made.
  3. Name the main colors and how they meet: soft edges, hard edges, overlaps.
  4. Read the title and date last. A title like "Composition" usually points to pure arrangement; a title naming a place or a thing hints that the work was abstracted from something real.

Tate notes that abstract art is often seen as standing for order, purity, simplicity and spirituality. You do not have to agree, but it explains why some abstract painters took their grids and color fields so seriously.

Where can you see abstract art in film and animation?

Abstraction moves well, so it found its way into animation early. The German-American animator Oskar Fischinger made abstract musical animation for decades, influenced Disney's Fantasia (1940), and made Motion Painting No. 1 (1947), which is listed on the U.S. National Film Registry.

Fantasia itself opens with Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D Minor. Live shots of the orchestra fade into abstract patterns, and animated lines, shapes and clouds follow the music. Disney hired Fischinger in late 1938 to work on that segment, but rejected his test reels, and he left the studio in October 1939, before it was finished.

In Canada, Norman McLaren and Evelyn Lambart made Begone Dull Care (1949) by painting and scratching directly onto film stock, turning Oscar Peterson's jazz into moving color. Abstraction also sits behind the flat geometry of Art Deco design, and the next big style after Abstract Expressionism, pop art, turned back to recognizable images on purpose. Questions people ask

What is abstract art in simple words?

It is art that does not show any recognizable thing. There is no person, landscape or object to find. The picture is made of colors, shapes, lines and brush marks, and those are the subject. Some abstract works start from a real thing and simplify it; others never had a real thing behind them.

Who invented abstract art?

No single person. Wassily Kandinsky was long described as the first modern artist to paint purely abstract pictures, in 1910–11. Britannica notes that this story was later questioned because the Swedish artist Hilma af Klint painted her first abstract work in 1906. Malevich, Mondrian, Delaunay and Tatlin also moved to abstraction before 1914.

What is the difference between abstract art and Abstract Expressionism?

Abstract art is the broad category: any art without recognizable objects. Abstract Expressionism is one movement inside it, made by American painters such as Jackson Pollock, Mark Rothko and Willem de Kooning in the 1940s and 1950s, known for gestural brushstrokes and a look of spontaneity.

Is abstract art the same as modern art?

No. Modern art covers many styles, including ones full of recognizable figures, like Surrealism and pop art. Tate says abstract art has formed a central stream of modern art since the early 1900s, so it is one large part of modern art, not all of it.

Why do some people dislike abstract art?

Mostly because there is nothing to recognize, so the usual question, what is it a picture of, has no answer. Britannica notes that abstract art puzzled and confused many people. Looking at it for color, rhythm and scale instead of a subject usually helps.
