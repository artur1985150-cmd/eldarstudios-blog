Manga are comics that originate in Japan. Most are printed in black and white and read from right to left, and they are made for every age group, from children to adults. In Japan the word covers comics and cartooning of any kind; outside Japan it means Japanese comics specifically. Many manga are later turned into anime.

What does the word manga mean?

Manga is written with two Japanese characters: man, meaning "whimsical or impromptu," and ga, meaning "pictures." The same root gives the Korean word for comics, manhwa, and the Chinese manhua. Cambridge Dictionary's short English definition is "Japanese comic books that tell stories in pictures."

The word came into common use in 1798 with Santō Kyōden's picture book Shiji no yukikai. It spread through the Hokusai Manga, sketch books by the ukiyo-e artist Hokusai published between 1814 and 1834. Those were collections of drawings, not stories. The cartoonist Rakuten Kitazawa (1876-1955) was the first to use "manga" in the modern sense.

How do you recognize manga art?

Manga art is usually black and white, with expressive faces and panels that move like a film camera. Dictionary.com notes the impressionistic backgrounds and big-eyed characters that Western readers associate with the style. Styles vary a lot between artists, from soft romance to gritty, realistic drama.

Things to look for on a page:

  • black ink on white paper, with full color rare;
  • panels that cut from wide shots to extreme close-ups, a "cinematographic" technique Osamu Tezuka helped spread;
  • a shared set of visual symbols, such as sweat drops or anger veins;
  • small, big-headed comic versions of characters in jokes, called chibi;
  • page order from right to left.

Wikipedia gives three reasons for the black and white: tight deadlines, artistic effect and low printing costs.

A short history of manga

Manga's visual roots go back to illustrated scrolls of the 12th century, but modern manga grew in the years after the Second World War. Tezuka's story manga, long series with a film-like flow, set the pattern. Weekly magazines in the 1950s and 1960s turned manga into a mass medium.

How is manga published?

Most manga first appear as chapters in a magazine, weekly or monthly, alongside many other series. Popular series are then collected into paperback books called tankōbon. If a series is popular enough, it may be adapted into an anime during or after its run. Artists often start with one-shot stories, and the ones that do well are continued.

The magazines are thick anthologies of 200 to more than 850 pages, printed on low-quality newsprint. Each series gets roughly 20 to 40 pages an issue. Readers who want to catch up buy the collected volumes, which use better paper. A manga artist, called a mangaka, usually works with a few assistants and an editor from the publisher.

Today a growing share is read digitally. Of Japan's ¥704.3 billion manga market in 2024, about ¥500 billion was digital and about ¥200 billion print.

What do shonen, shojo, seinen and josei mean?

They are publishing categories named after the audience of the magazine where a series runs. Shonen magazines target adolescent boys, shojo magazines girls, seinen magazines young adult men and josei magazines adult women. The label tells you where a story was published, not its genre.

Real readers cross these lines all the time. A 2006 survey cited on Wikipedia found Weekly Shōnen Jump was the most popular manga magazine among female readers. Dragon Ball, Naruto, Bleach, One Piece and Slam Dunk all came out of Weekly Shōnen Jump.

How do you read manga?

Start from what an English reader would call the back of the book, and read each page from the top right corner, the way Japanese text runs. After a few pages it feels normal.

  1. Open the book with the spine on your right.
  2. Read the panels on each page from right to left, then move down to the next row.
  3. Inside a panel, read the speech balloons from right to left and top to bottom.

For a first series, pick a genre you already like. Fantasy readers might try an isekai series; readers who prefer mature drama can look at seinen titles. Korean comics follow different rules, explained in our guide to manhwa, and more on comics in general is in our comics section.

Questions people ask

What does the word manga mean?

It is written with two characters: man, meaning whimsical or impromptu, and ga, meaning pictures. The word came into common use in 1798 and spread through the Hokusai Manga sketch books (1814-1834). The cartoonist Rakuten Kitazawa first used it in the modern sense of comics.

Why is manga read from right to left?

Because Japanese is traditionally written and read that way, and manga follows the page order of Japanese books. So you start at what an English reader would call the back cover.

Why is most manga black and white?

Wikipedia gives three reasons: tight weekly and monthly deadlines, artistic choice, since color can weaken the impact of the line work, and lower printing costs. Some full-color manga exist, such as Colorful.

Is manga the same as anime?

No. Manga is comics; anime is animation. Many anime are adaptations of manga, so the same story often exists in both forms, usually with the manga coming first and running further ahead.

What is the difference between manga and manhwa?

Manga is from Japan, manhwa is from Korea, and manhua is from China. All three words come from the same root for whimsical pictures.
