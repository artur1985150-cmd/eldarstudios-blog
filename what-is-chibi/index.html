In short

  • Chibi, also called super deformed or SD, is a style that draws characters small and chubby, with oversized heads and eyes and stubby limbs.
  • A normal anime character is about five to eight heads tall; a chibi is usually two to four heads tall, sometimes with a head as big as the body.
  • The style grew out of 1980s fan art and toys; SD Gundam capsule toys, launched by Bandai in 1985, made the name super deformed common.
  • In Japanese, chibi is everyday slang for a short person or small child and can be an insult.

Chibi is an art style from Japanese anime and manga that draws characters tiny and chubby, with oversized heads and eyes, stubby arms and legs and very little detail. It is also called super deformed, or SD. A normal anime character is about five to eight heads tall; a chibi is usually two to four. Shows switch into chibi for jokes and cute moments, and merchandise uses it constantly.

What does chibi mean?

In Japanese, chibi is everyday slang for something small: a short person, a small child, a runt. Fans use it for the style because the characters look like small, childish versions of themselves. The full Japanese term is chibi kyara, "chibi character," with kyara borrowed from the English word character.

The word is old. Tofugu traces it to the verb chibiru, "to wear down," used for worn-out sandals and brushes that had lost their hairs. A related form appears in the Taiheiki, a historical epic from the 1370s. In the Meiji period (1868-1912), people began using chibi on its own, and during the Second World War it settled into the meaning "small and short."

How do you recognize chibi art?

In chibi art the head takes up one third to one half of the character's whole height, and the eyes take up much of the face. The body shrinks to a small, rounded shape with short limbs. Details like jacket folds disappear, but a character's signature feature, such as wild hair or a favorite accessory, gets bigger.

Height in heads About 5 to 8 About 2 to 4, sometimes 1

Eyes Often large Oversized
Limbs Realistic length Short and stubby
Detail Clothing folds, shading Simple shapes, few details

The animator Gen Sato, author of a how-to-draw book on super deformed characters, set the head-to-body ratio between 1:1 and 4:1, according to Tofugu. He also noted that the facial features tend to gather toward the center of the face.

Why do anime characters suddenly turn chibi?

A sudden switch to chibi is a visual punchline. Dictionary.com describes the style as a way to emphasize changes in mood or call attention to the punchline of a joke. Wikipedia's anime article lists chibi among the recurring sight gags used to show confusion or embarrassment.

It works because the audience knows the normal design. When a serious hero shrinks into a round little figure for two seconds, the scene tells you, without words, that the moment is not to be taken seriously. Then the character snaps back to normal.

Where did the chibi style come from?

The chibi style grew out of anime fandom in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Fan magazines such as Monthly OUT printed short, cute parody manga of hit science fiction like Space Battleship Yamato. Professionals picked it up from there, and toy companies turned it into a product line.

Tofugu, citing Gen Sato, gives these dates:

Year What happened
1982 Sato's chibi gag comic about Alfin, from the OVA Crusher Joe, appears in Animage magazine in October
1983 A chibi Alfin manga runs in Monthly OUT; soon after, Kaiyodo makes the first chibi figure
1984 Bandai's toy department notices Koji Yokoi's chibi Gundam fan art
1985 Bandai launches SD Gundam World capsule toys, and the term super deformed enters Japanese

Yokoi based his small Gundams on the mini Godzilla and mini Ultraman that Akira Toriyama drew in Dr. Slump. Some people at Bandai felt the cute Gundams hurt the integrity of a serious franchise. The toys were a hit anyway, and "chibi kyara" followed "super deformed" into the language soon after.

How did chibi reach English-speaking fans?

Mostly through Sailor Moon. Its character Chibiusa, a pet name for the daughter of Sailor Moon built from Chibi Usagi, "little rabbit," brought the word to American fans in the mid-1990s, according to Wikipedia. Dictionary.com also credits the 1990s anime and manga Sailor Moon with spreading the popular sense of chibi in English.

Since then the style has moved beyond Japan. Wikipedia lists chibi moments in anime-influenced Western series such as Teen Titans and Avatar: The Last Airbender, and in the webcomic Homestuck. In English, fans use chibi as a noun, an adjective and even a verb: to chibify a character means to redraw them in chibi form.

Is chibi a rude word in Japanese?

It can be. Calling an adult chibi is a bit like calling them "shorty" or "small fry": fine between friends, hurtful from a stranger. Japan's Kōjien dictionary, quoted by Tofugu, says the word is used either to make light of someone or as a term of affection. As an art term, it carries no insult.

Tofugu reports that in 1983 NHK, Japan's public broadcaster, added chibi to the list of words its presenters should not say on air, together with the cuter chibikko, and that NHK still classes it as discriminatory. So "chibi art" is safe to say to anyone, while "you're so chibi" is best kept for people who know you well.

Where can you see chibi in anime and manga?

Almost everywhere. Tofugu notes that nearly every popular character, from One Piece to Dragon Ball, has an SD version, official or made by fans. You will find chibi in comic relief scenes of anime, in manga and, above all, in merchandise such as figures and capsule toys.

The style belongs to the wider Japanese idea of kawaii, or cuteness, which is one reason it sells so well as merchandise. For the bigger picture of how chibi fits into anime drawing, start with our guide to what anime is, browse other art styles, or see more articles in our anime section.

Questions people ask

What does chibi mean in Japanese?

Chibi is a colloquial word for a small or short person, a small child or a runt. It comes from the verb chibiru, 'to wear down,' once used for worn sandals and brushes. Depending on tone, it can be affectionate or insulting, much like 'shorty' in English.

Is chibi the same as super deformed?

Yes, in practice. Super deformed, or SD, is the older industry term, popularized by Bandai's SD Gundam toys in 1985. Chibi kyara, 'chibi character,' followed soon after. English-speaking fans mostly say chibi, and the two words describe the same style.

How many heads tall is a chibi?

Usually two to four heads tall. The animator Gen Sato, quoted by Tofugu, put the head-to-body ratio between 1:1 and 4:1. Wikipedia describes the head as one third to one half of the character's height.

Is it rude to call someone chibi?

It can be. Japan's Kōjien dictionary says the word is used either to make light of someone or as a term of affection. Tofugu reports that NHK, Japan's public broadcaster, added chibi to its list of words not to say on air in 1983.
