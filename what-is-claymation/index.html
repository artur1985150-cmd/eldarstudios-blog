In short

  • Claymation is a kind of stop motion in which figures made of clay or plasticine are moved by hand between photographs.
  • The word joins clay and animation; Will Vinton used it as the title of a late-1970s documentary and later trademarked it.
  • Aardman in Bristol made the style world-famous with Morph, Wallace & Gromit, Chicken Run and Shaun the Sheep; Nick Park won four Academy Awards.
  • The oldest known surviving clay film is Long Live the Bull (1926); Gumby arrived on television in 1955.

Claymation is a type of stop-motion animation in which characters and sets are made of clay, usually plasticine. An animator shapes a figure, photographs it, moves or reshapes it slightly, and photographs it again. Played back at film speed, the clay seems to walk, talk and change shape by itself. The best-known examples are Aardman's Wallace & Gromit, Chicken Run and Shaun the Sheep, along with Gumby and Pingu.

What does claymation mean?

Claymation means animation made with clay figures that are moved frame by frame. Other names for it are clay animation and plasticine animation. It is one branch of stop motion, the wider technique of photographing real objects between small changes. What sets claymation apart is that the material itself is soft and can be reshaped.

That softness matters on screen. A clay figure can stretch, squash, melt or turn into something else between two frames, which a jointed puppet cannot. Animators sometimes split the style in two. In "character" claymation, as in the films of Art Clokey and Will Vinton, the figure stays recognizable throughout a shot. In "freeform" claymation, as in the films of Eli Noyes, the clay changes shape radically as the animation runs.

Why is it called claymation?

The word is a blend of clay and animation. Will Vinton, an animator in Portland, Oregon, made a documentary in the late 1970s called Claymation about his studio's technique, and he later trademarked the word. That is why careful writers and some studios use "clay animation" instead.

Sources disagree by a year on the documentary. Wikipedia's article on Vinton lists a 17-minute film from 1978, while its stop motion article says 1977. Either way, the trademark was meant to set his team's work apart from other clay films.

Vinton had earned the right to name it. With the sculptor Bob Gardiner he made Closed Mondays (1974), an 8-minute clay short about a drunk who wanders into a closed art museum. It took 14 months to make and won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film, the first stop-motion film to win an Oscar. His studio later made The California Raisins commercials and the CBS special Will Vinton's Claymation Christmas Celebration (1987), which won a Primetime Emmy.

How is claymation made?

Each character is sculpted from plasticine, usually around a wire skeleton called an armature, and placed on a miniature set. The animator takes one picture, moves the figure a tiny amount by hand, and takes the next. Film runs at 24 frames per second, so this repeats thousands of times.

The numbers add up fast. Most clay films shoot "on twos", holding each pose for two frames, so 12 changes make one second of film. Wikipedia's claymation article puts the total at about 21,600 changes for a 30-minute film and 64,800 for a 90-minute feature.

Plasticine is the usual material for a reason. William Harbutt developed it in 1897, and unlike ordinary clay it does not dry and harden. That lets animators rework a face for days. The downside is that clay picks up every smudge, hair and speck of dust, and humid air can soften figures and shift a set. Animators call a set mid-shot a "hot set" that nobody may touch, and they try to finish a scene in a day.

How can you recognize claymation on screen?

Look at the surfaces. Clay has a soft, slightly shiny skin, often with faint fingerprints and tool marks that change a little from frame to frame. Mouths and eyebrows are reshaped rather than swapped. Characters tend to have rounded, chunky forms, and the light on them comes from real studio lamps.

A few clues separate clay from its neighbors:

  1. Puppet stop motion (like The Nightmare Before Christmas) has cloth costumes and hard faces; clay figures are smooth and soft all over.
  2. Computer graphics can imitate clay. Aardman's Flushed Away is a CGI copy of the claymation look, so a too-perfect surface is a hint.
  3. Big features often switch materials. Wikipedia notes that feature-length productions have generally moved from clay to silicone rubber and resin parts, while keeping a clay look. Will Vinton called one foam-rubber process "Foamation".

Clay has also made its way into adult TV and games. Robot Chicken mixes claymation with action figures, Moral Orel ran on Adult Swim, and The Neverhood and ClayFighter were built from clay models.

Where did claymation come from?

Claymation is almost as old as film animation. Clay figures shaped themselves on screen in trick films of 1908, such as Segundo de Chomón's Sculpteur Moderne and The Sculptor's Nightmare. By the 1920s cel drawing dominated American studios, and clay stayed on the edges until television brought it back.

The early decades left only fragments. Willie Hopkins made more than fifty clay segments called Miracles in Mud for the Universal Screen Magazine between 1916 and 1918. Helena Smith Dayton, possibly the first female animator, screened clay shorts in 1917, but none of her films has been found. The Fleischer brothers slipped clay into eight shots of an Out of the Inkwell cartoon in 1921. Joseph Sunn's Long Live the Bull (1926) is the oldest surviving film made mainly in clay.

Art Clokey changed that in 1955. His student film Gumbasia set shapes moving to jazz, and Gumby followed, first in segments of Howdy Doody and then in his own series. In the 1970s Will Vinton's Oscar and the founding of Aardman in Britain put clay on the map for good.

Who are Aardman and why do they matter for claymation?

Aardman is the Bristol studio that made claymation famous worldwide. Peter Lord and David Sproxton started it in 1972 as a low-budget project. Its name comes from a "hard man" superhero character they animated for Vision On, a BBC series for deaf children, and its clay character Morph followed.

Nick Park joined in 1985 and became the most successful claymation director. He received six Academy Award nominations and won four, for Creature Comforts, The Wrong Trousers, A Close Shave and Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit. Park and Aardman also helped make the award-winning music video for Peter Gabriel's "Sledgehammer" in 1986, and Chicken Run (2000), which became the highest-grossing stop-motion animated film.

The studio is still busy. Aardman counts its 50 years from its first production in 1976, and in June 2026 the Royal Mint issued a 50p coin for the anniversary, with Morph, Shaun the Sheep, Feathers McGraw and Wallace and Gromit on it.

Questions people ask

Why is it called claymation?

It is a blend of clay and animation. Will Vinton, the Portland animator behind Closed Mondays and The California Raisins, made a documentary in the late 1970s titled Claymation about his studio's clay technique. He later trademarked the word. Neutral terms are clay animation and plasticine animation.

Is claymation the same as stop motion?

Claymation is one type of stop motion. All claymation is stop motion, but not all stop motion is claymation. Stop motion also uses jointed puppets, paper cutouts, toys and live actors. The Nightmare Before Christmas is puppet stop motion; Wallace & Gromit is claymation.

What kind of clay is used in claymation?

Usually plasticine, an oil-based modeling clay that William Harbutt developed in 1897. It does not dry and harden like ordinary clay, so it can be reshaped for weeks. Figures are often sculpted around a wire skeleton called an armature.

How long does it take to make a claymation film?

A very long time. Shooting on twos means 12 changes for each second of film. Wikipedia's estimate is about 21,600 changes for a 30-minute film and 64,800 for a 90-minute feature. Will Vinton and Bob Gardiner's 8-minute short Closed Mondays took 14 months.
