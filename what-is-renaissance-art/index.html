In short

  • Renaissance art is the painting, sculpture and decorative art of the European Renaissance, which took classical Greek and Roman art as its model.
  • It emerged as a distinct style in Italy around 1400, with Florence at the center; Wikipedia gives the whole span as 1350 to 1620.
  • Its tools were linear perspective, careful study of anatomy, light and shadow, and, from about 1475 in Italy, oil paint.
  • The High Renaissance of Leonardo, Michelangelo and Raphael was short: about 1495 to 1520 by Wikipedia's dates, 1500 to 1530 by Tate's.
  • After it came Mannerism, then the Baroque.

Renaissance art is the painting, sculpture and decorative art Europe made from roughly 1400 to 1600, when artists took ancient Greek and Roman art as their model. It began in Italy, with Florence at the center, and spread across Europe. Its painters formalized linear perspective, studied anatomy and light, and made pictures look like windows onto real space. The best-known names are Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo and Raphael.

What does Renaissance mean?

Renaissance is French for "rebirth". Tate defines it in English as the revival of art in Italy that began around 1400, under the influence of rediscovered classical art and culture. What was reborn, in this view, was the art and culture of the classical world.

The wider period carries the same name. Wikipedia says it is variously defined as covering the 15th and 16th centuries or, more broadly, a longer span into the 17th. Its intellectual side was humanism, which Wikipedia glosses as the movement to recover, interpret and assimilate the language, literature, learning and values of ancient Greece and Rome.

Tate puts the High Renaissance at about 1500 to 1530. Wikipedia ends it in 1520: Raphael's death that year, at 37, is considered by many art historians to be its end. Wikipedia's overall span for Renaissance art is 1350 to 1620.

The Early Renaissance has a neat starting point: the 1401 competition for the bronze doors of the Florence Baptistery, which Lorenzo Ghiberti won.

What are the main characteristics of Renaissance art?

Renaissance painting aims at a more realistic picture of the world. Five techniques do much of the work, and each has a name you will meet on museum labels.

  • Linear perspective. A way of drawing space realistically. Giotto was first to treat a painting as a window into space; Filippo Brunelleschi's demonstrations and Leon Battista Alberti's writings made perspective a formal technique.
  • Foreshortening. Shortening lines in a drawing to create an illusion of depth.
  • Chiaroscuro. A strong contrast between light and dark that gives the illusion of depth. Art historian Marcia B. Hall counts it among four modes of painting available to High Renaissance painters.
  • Sfumato. A term coined by Leonardo for softening sharp outlines by blending one tone gradually into another with thin glazes. It comes from the Italian for "to fade out".
  • Oil paint. Improved by Belgian and French artists such as Robert Campin and Jan van Eyck, and adopted in Italy from about 1475. Leonardo adopted it as his primary medium.

Behind all of them sits a closer look at nature. Wikipedia lists an increased awareness of nature and a revival of classical learning among the forces behind the art. Leonardo's dissection of cadavers advanced the understanding of skeletal and muscular anatomy.

How is Renaissance art different from medieval art?

For art historians, Wikipedia says, Renaissance art marks Europe's move from the medieval period to the Early Modern age. The break was gradual.

French painters such as Jean Hey combined realism with a medieval formality that included gilt backgrounds. Netherlandish painters kept a medieval view of hierarchical proportion and religious symbolism.

Giotto, working around 1300, is the usual starting point. Wikipedia's article on the Renaissance credits him with first treating a painting "as a window into space", and his Lamentation in the Scrovegni Chapel (c. 1305) is said to foreshadow the new style.

Who were the most important Renaissance artists?

The three giants are Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo and Raphael, all active in the High Renaissance around 1500. Around them stood many others across Italy and Northern Europe.

In Italy before 1500: Masaccio, Donatello, Brunelleschi, Paolo Uccello, Fra Angelico, Piero della Francesca and Sandro Botticelli. In Venice: Giovanni Bellini, Giorgione and Titian. In the Netherlands: Jan van Eyck, Robert Campin, Rogier van der Weyden, Hugo van der Goes and Hieronymus Bosch. In Germany: Albrecht Dürer (1471–1528), Hans Holbein the Younger, Matthias Grünewald and Lucas Cranach the Elder. In France: Jean Fouquet.

What was the Northern Renaissance?

The Renaissance in Northern Europe is called the Northern Renaissance. Its art is best known from the Netherlands and Germany.

Netherlandish painting developed partly independently of Italian painting. Its painters did not build a picture on linear perspective and correct proportion. They kept hierarchical proportion and religious symbolism, and took pleasure in realistic treatment of natural and man-made things.

Dürer is the bridge: Wikipedia credits him with bringing Italian styles and ideas to Germany. Britain came very late and relied largely on imported painters.

What came after the Renaissance?

Mannerism took over between 1520 and 1530. Tate describes it as a 16th-century style of artificiality, elegance and sensuous distortion of the human figure. Tate calls the Baroque the dominant style of the 17th century. Its deep shadows are covered in our guides to chiaroscuro and Baroque art.

The Renaissance lingered. Tate notes that art academies held up Raphael as the prime model until the mid-19th century, when artists turned their backs on that classical approach, as in the Pre-Raphaelites, realism, naturalism and impressionism.

How to recognize a Renaissance painting in a museum

You can often tell before you reach the label:

  • The space recedes in perspective, with floor tiles, buildings or roads running toward one point.
  • Figures have solid form, natural faces and gestures, and studied anatomy.
  • Light and dark model the forms. Strong chiaroscuro became popular only in Mannerism and the Baroque, and a figure picked out of deep shadow, called tenebrism, is Caravaggio's Baroque territory.
  • The subject is often religious, but myths and history from the classical world also appear.

Questions people ask

What is Renaissance art in simple words?

It is the art Europe made when painters and sculptors went back to ancient Greek and Roman art for models, roughly from 1400 to 1600. Pictures started to look like windows onto real space, with more natural bodies, depth and light.

What does Renaissance mean?

Renaissance is French for rebirth. Tate explains that in English it names the revival of art in Italy from about 1400, driven by the rediscovery of classical art and culture.

Who are the most famous Renaissance artists?

Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo and Raphael, the three names tied to the High Renaissance. Earlier Italian names include Giotto, Masaccio, Donatello and Botticelli and Jan van Eyck in the Netherlands. Titian worked in Venice, and Albrecht Dürer carried Italian ideas to Germany.

What is the difference between Renaissance and Baroque art?

They are neighbors in time. Renaissance art covers roughly 1400 to 1600; Tate calls the Baroque the dominant style of the 17th century. Light is one visible difference: chiaroscuro began in the Renaissance but came into wide use in the Baroque, where tenebrism, its extreme form, took it further.

Is the Mona Lisa Renaissance art?

Yes. Leonardo da Vinci painted it in 1503–1506, during the High Renaissance. Wikipedia names it as an example of how Leonardo's oil paint showed light and its effects on landscape and objects.
