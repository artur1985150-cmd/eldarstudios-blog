In short

  • Art Deco is a design style of the 1920s and 1930s known for sleek geometric or stylized forms and manufactured materials such as Bakelite and chrome.
  • It takes its name from the 1925 Paris Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes; at the time it was called style moderne.
  • Its sources were wide: Art Nouveau, Cubism, the Bauhaus, the Ballets Russes, and ancient Egyptian and Central American art.
  • The Chrysler Building (1930), the Empire State Building and Rockefeller Center are its best-known monuments; Miami Beach has a whole Art Deco district.
  • Animation has borrowed it for decades, from Disney's Fantasia and Sleeping Beauty to Batman: The Animated Series and the BioShock games.

Art Deco is a design style of the 1920s and 1930s built on sleek geometric shapes, stylized ornament and glossy modern materials. It began in western Europe in the 1910s and 1920s, was first shown as a whole at a Paris exhibition in 1925, and became a major style in the United States in the 1930s. You know it from the Chrysler Building's sunburst spire, from old cinema facades, and from posters full of zigzags and streamlined trains.

What is Art Deco in simple words?

Art Deco is the modern look of the years between the two world wars. Britannica describes it as a style of sleek geometric or stylized forms that often used manufactured materials, and calls it modernism turned into fashion. It covered almost everything: buildings, furniture, jewelry, glass, fashion, posters and train design.

It was made for two markets at once. Some pieces were handmade luxury objects; others were mass-produced. Either way, Britannica says, the aim was a sleek, anti-traditional elegance that signaled wealth and sophistication. That is why Art Deco still reads as glamorous on screen: it was designed to look expensive.

Where does the name Art Deco come from?

The name comes from the Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes, an exhibition of modern decorative and industrial arts held in Paris in 1925, where the style was first shown as a whole. At the time people did not say "Art Deco." The V&A notes it was then known as style moderne.

The short name came much later. The V&A says the term was coined in the 1960s. It became a widely used label in 1968, when the historian Bevis Hillier published the first major book on the style, Art Deco of the 20s and 30s; he noted that dealers were already using the phrase. In Britain and America in the 1920s, critics also used "Moderne," "Jazz Moderne" and "Zigzag Moderne."

The 1925 exhibition brought together thousands of designers from Europe and beyond. Two of them, the furniture maker Émile-Jacques Ruhlmann and the glass designer René Lalique, became leading names of early Art Deco.

What does Art Deco look like?

You can spot Art Deco by simple, clean shapes, often with a streamlined look; ornament that is geometric or stylized from real forms; and unusual, often costly materials. Britannica lists Bakelite and other plastics, vita-glass and ferroconcrete alongside jade, silver, ivory, obsidian, chrome and rock crystal.

A quick checklist for a building, a poster or a film set:

  1. Straight lines and repeated geometric patterns: zigzags, chevrons, stepped outlines.
  2. Sun rays and fan shapes, like the Chrysler Building's spire.
  3. Symmetry and repetition of the same element, borrowed from the look of machines.
  4. Figures, animals and leaves drawn in a stylized, simplified way rather than realistically.
  5. Shiny surfaces: chrome, lacquer, glass, polished stone.
  6. Streamlining, the smooth "bullet" shape of 1930s trains and cars.

Britannica explains the machine look directly: the style admired the modernity of the machine and the qualities of machine-made objects, such as simplicity, flat planes, symmetry and unvaried repetition. Typical motifs included nude female figures, animals, foliage and sun rays, all in conventionalized forms.

What influenced Art Deco?

Art Deco borrowed from almost everything around it. Britannica names Art Nouveau, the Bauhaus, Cubism and Serge Diaghilev's Ballets Russes as formative influences, with decorative ideas from American Indian, Egyptian and early classical art and from nature. The V&A calls it less one style than a pastiche of many.

Ancient cultures were a big source. The V&A points to the excavation of Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922, which set off a wave of Egyptian imagery: lotus flowers, scarabs, hieroglyphs, pylons and pyramids. Tate adds Aztec and other ancient Central American art, plus the design of modern ships, trains and cars.

The avant-garde mattered as much. In the 1920s designers absorbed Fauvism, Cubism, Futurism, De Stijl, Suprematism and Constructivism, often lumped together as "Cubism," according to the V&A. Tate says Cubism is the main thing separating Art Deco from Art Nouveau. Some of that geometry comes straight from abstract art, which was being invented in the same years.

How is Art Deco different from Art Nouveau?

Art Nouveau curves; Art Deco squares up. Tate describes Art Nouveau as a style of the 1890s built on sinuous lines and flowing plant shapes. Art Deco, which followed it, kept some plant forms but made them more fragmented and geometric under the influence of Cubism.

The line between them is not always sharp. Tate points out that curving plant imagery lived on in some Art Deco design, such as the work of Clarice Cliff in Britain.

What are the most famous Art Deco buildings and designers?

The best-known monuments are in New York. Britannica names Rockefeller Center, the Chrysler Building by William Van Alen and the Empire State Building by Shreve, Lamb & Harmon as the most monumental Art Deco works. The Chrysler Building, with its sunburst spire, was completed in 1930.

In the 1930s the style took over South Beach in Miami, Florida, which is now the Art Deco historic district. Tate adds that Art Deco was a notable style for cinemas and hotels.

Among designers, Britannica lists the furniture makers Jacques Ruhlmann and Maurice Dufrène, the glass and jewelry designer René Lalique, the fashion designer Erté, the metalsmith Jean Puiforcat and the sculptor Chiparus. The fashion designer Paul Poiret and the poster artist Edward McKnight Kauffer brought the style to a mass audience.

Where do you see Art Deco in films, cartoons and games?

Art Deco is one of animation's favorite borrowed styles. Disney's Fantasia and Sleeping Beauty, Batman: The Animated Series, and the cartoon Falling Hare (1943), with its Art Deco skyscrapers, are all cited for Art Deco visuals, along with shorts like Night Hood and Miss Glory.

The style also shapes game worlds. The underwater city of Rapture in the BioShock series is built in Art Deco architecture, and the design of Iacon City in the animated film Transformers One drew on Art Deco visuals. Going the other way, expressionist theater and film, illustrated by Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927), are listed among the influences on Art Deco itself.

Why does it suit the screen? It gives a city instant period and instant glamour, and its strong outlines and repeated patterns read clearly at a glance. The same is true of pop art, which later borrowed the bold graphics of print. The list of art styles puts Art Deco next to its neighbors, and the painting and graphics section collects more guides.

When did Art Deco end?

Art Deco faded in the 1930s and was out of fashion in most places by the Second World War. The V&A traces the decline to the 1929 stock market crash: by the mid-1930s it was mocked as a gaudy, false image of luxury, and by the war the hostility was intense.

It came back. Britannica notes renewed interest from the late 1960s, the same years the name Art Deco caught on, and says the style kept inspiring decorative art, fashion and jewelry into the 21st century. Some designs never left: the V&A points to Poul Henningsen's PH lamp of 1927, still in production with minor changes.

Questions people ask

What is Art Deco in simple words?

It is the look of the 1920s and 1930s: buildings, furniture, posters and jewelry made of clean geometric shapes, zigzags, sunbursts and stepped outlines, often in shiny or expensive materials. Britannica calls it modernism turned into fashion, made to look sleek, modern and wealthy.

Why is it called Art Deco?

The name comes from the 1925 Paris exhibition of modern decorative and industrial arts, the Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes. People at the time said style moderne. The short name Art Deco was coined in the 1960s and spread after Bevis Hillier's 1968 book on the style.

What is the difference between Art Deco and Art Nouveau?

Art Nouveau came first, in the 1890s, and is built on long curving lines and plant shapes. Art Deco followed in the 1910s and 1920s and, shaped by Cubism, turned those into straighter, more geometric patterns. Tate calls Art Deco both a successor to Art Nouveau and a reaction against it.

Is the Empire State Building Art Deco?

Yes. Britannica names it, along with the Chrysler Building and Rockefeller Center in New York, as the most monumental examples of Art Deco. It was designed by the firm Shreve, Lamb & Harmon; the Chrysler Building, completed in 1930, was designed by William Van Alen.

When did Art Deco end?

The 1929 stock market crash began its decline. By the mid-1930s critics called it gaudy, and by the Second World War it had gone out of fashion in most places. Interest returned from the late 1960s, and Britannica says it still inspires decorative art, fashion and jewelry.
