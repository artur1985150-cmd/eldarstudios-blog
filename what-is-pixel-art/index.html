In short

  • Pixel art is digital art where each pixel is placed on purpose, usually on a small canvas with a short list of colors.
  • It grew out of 1970s and 1980s arcade games and consoles; the term was first published in 1982 by Adele Goldberg and Robert Flegal of Xerox PARC.
  • Today it is a deliberate choice: Stardew Valley, Undertale and Celeste chose the look long after hardware stopped forcing it.
  • Pixel art shows up in games, icons, animated loops, music videos and street mosaics, and MoMA added Pac-Man to its collection in 2012.

Pixel art is a kind of digital art in which the picture is built from pixels, the tiny squares of a screen, and each one is placed on purpose. Pixel artists usually work on a small canvas with a short list of colors, so every square counts. The style comes from the arcade games and home consoles of the late 1970s and 1980s, and artists still choose it today for games, animation and prints.

What is pixel art in simple words?

Pixel art is a picture drawn square by square, like a mosaic made on a computer. The artist picks the color of each pixel by hand instead of letting software blur or smooth the image. Change a few pixels and the whole face of a character can change.

The word pixel is short for "picture element", the smallest dot of color on a screen. TheCollector traces the word to 1965, when the scientist Fred Billingsley used it in a paper on digital video processing at JPL. Every image on a phone is made of pixels, of course. What makes pixel art different is that you are meant to see them.

Japanese artists often call the same thing "dot art". In games, drawing characters and objects this way is called "spriting", from sprite, the small two-dimensional image that a game moves around the screen.

How can you recognize pixel art style?

Look for hard square edges, a small number of flat colors and no soft blur. Curves turn into little staircases. Shading is done with a few steps of color or with checkerboard patterns rather than smooth gradients. Enlarged, the picture still looks crisp, just blocky.

A few details tell you that an artist placed the pixels by hand:

  • Outlines are one pixel wide and clean, with no stray gray dots around them.
  • The palette is small and repeats. Classic limits were 4 colors on the Game Boy and 16 on the Commodore 64.
  • Dithering, a checkerboard or striped pattern of two colors, stands in for a third color seen from a distance.
  • Edges are softened by hand with a pixel or two of an in-between color, a trick called manual anti-aliasing.
  • Characters move in short loops of a few frames, like a flip book.

A photo shrunk down, or a picture run through a "pixelate" filter, fails most of these tests. Its colors are muddy and random, and nobody chose them. There is no fixed line, though. The pixel art article on Wikipedia notes that an image counts as pixel art when deliberate thought went into each pixel, and that the point where a higher-resolution picture stops being pixel art is subjective.

Where did pixel art come from?

Pixel art began as a necessity. Early arcade machines and consoles could show only a few pixels and colors, so designers had to plan every square. Space Invaders (1978) and Pac-Man (1980), and 8-bit consoles like the Nintendo Entertainment System (1983), set the look most people now call retro.

There were digital pixel pictures earlier. Richard Shoup's SuperPaint system at Xerox PARC dates to 1972. The term "pixel art" was first published in 1982, in a journal letter by Adele Goldberg and Robert Flegal, also of Xerox PARC.

In the 1980s, professional artists joined the programmers. Sierra released Mystery House and the King's Quest series, and Lucasfilm Games made Maniac Mansion. In the late 1980s the European demoscene, groups who made audiovisual demos on the Commodore 64, ZX Spectrum and Atari ST, started making pixel art for its own sake.

TheCollector points out one more piece of history: old pixel art was made for CRT televisions. Their slight blur softened the jagged edges, so the same sprite can look harsher on a sharp modern screen.

What hardware limits shaped the classic look?

The classic look comes from two limits: screen resolution and number of colors. Artists still copy these numbers on purpose, the way a poet picks a sonnet's fourteen lines. The table shows three well-known machines.

Machine Resolution Colors
Game Boy 160 × 144 pixels 4
ZX Spectrum (original) 256 × 192 pixels 15
Commodore 64 320 × 200 pixels 16

In the 1990s these limits loosened. The Super Nintendo could show colors from a set of 32,768, and PC graphics reached millions. 3D games began to push hand-drawn sprites aside, and in the 2000s most big studios left pixel art behind.

Why do modern games still use pixel art?

Because the look is now a choice. Without hardware limits, a pixel style signals a certain feeling and keeps art work manageable for small teams. In the 2010s a wave of "retro" games made pixel art popular again, and the trend has held in the 2020s.

Some of the best-known titles from that wave: Terraria (2011), Fez (2012), Papers, Please (2013), Shovel Knight (2014), Undertale (2015), Stardew Valley (2016), Owlboy (2016) and Celeste (2018). Later came Loop Hero (2021), Vampire Survivors (2022), Dave the Diver (2023) and Balatro (2024). Many of them mix low-resolution sprites with modern lighting, filters or crisp menus, so "pure" pixel art and pixel-inspired art overlap.

TheCollector names Stardew Valley and Undertale as examples of indie games that made pixel art part of their charm. Wikipedia adds two other reasons people give for the revival: nostalgia among players who grew up with the SNES, and the freedom of small studios working without big publishers.

What is pixel art used for besides games?

Outside games, pixel art turns up in animated loops and GIFs, avatars, icons, music videos, LED signs and art prints. It has also reached street art and museums. The look works anywhere a small, readable image is needed or a retro mood is wanted.

A few examples from the record. Ivan Dixon and Paul Robertson drew a pixel art version of The Simpsons' opening sequence that drew international attention. The French street artist Invader is known for pixel-style tile mosaics placed on walls around the world. Synthwave musicians have used pixel animation for music videos. In the late 1990s and 2000s, "pixel dolls" were a popular kind of avatar on forums and chat sites.

Museums took notice too. In 2012 the Museum of Modern Art in New York acquired 14 video games for its collection, including Pac-Man and Tetris, as WIRED reported. Curator Paola Antonelli said the games were chosen as examples of interaction design.

Is pixel art a type of animation?

Pixel art is a way of drawing images, and it becomes animation when those images are drawn frame by frame and played in order. A walking game character is usually a loop of a few hand-placed frames. That makes pixel animation a close cousin of hand-drawn cartoons, just on a tiny grid.

Wikipedia calls pixel art a broad medium rather than a single style. Inside it, artists imitate impressionism, pointillism or cross-stitch, and some genres have their own names: isometric scenes drawn at a slant that looks three-dimensional, sprites for platform and fighting games, and "demakes" that redraw modern games in a retro style. It is also different from oekaki, a small-canvas drawing style where the overall feel matters more than each pixel, which gives it a looser, jagged look.

Questions people ask

Is pixel art the same as 8-bit art?

Not exactly. 8-bit describes old hardware, such as the Nintendo Entertainment System or the Commodore 64, with tight limits on colors. Pixel art is the wider medium. Much pixel art imitates the 8-bit look, but 16-bit games and modern indie titles are pixel art too.

Is any low-resolution picture pixel art?

No. A blurry photo or a shrunken image is made of pixels, but it is not pixel art. The usual test is intent: an artist decided where each pixel goes and which color it gets. Clean outlines, no soft blur and a small, chosen palette are signs of that.

What is pixel art used for?

Mostly video games, from arcade classics to indie hits. It is also used for animated loops and GIFs, avatars, icons, LED displays, music videos and art prints. The French street artist Invader turns the same look into tile mosaics on city walls.

Why is pixel art still popular?

Part of it is nostalgia for 1980s and 1990s games. Part is practical: small sprites let a small team finish a game. And the limits themselves appeal to artists, the way mosaic or cross-stitch does. Pixel art games kept selling through the 2010s and 2020s.
