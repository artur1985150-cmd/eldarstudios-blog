In short

  • Cyberpunk is a subgenre of science fiction set in a near future where advanced technology sits beside poverty, crime and decay, often summed up as "high tech and low life".
  • The word comes from Bruce Bethke's short story "Cyberpunk", written in 1980 and published in Amazing Stories in 1983.
  • William Gibson's novel Neuromancer (1984) fixed the genre's ideas, and Ridley Scott's Blade Runner (1982) fixed its look on screen.
  • Japan gave the genre Akira (manga from 1982, film 1988) and Ghost in the Shell (1995), which in turn shaped The Matrix (1999).
  • Cyberpunk 2077 (2020) and Cyberpunk: Edgerunners (2022) both come from a 1988 tabletop game called Cyberpunk, not from the books.

Cyberpunk is a subgenre of science fiction set in a near future where advanced technology lives side by side with poverty, crime and decay. Its short formula is "high tech and low life": artificial intelligence, body implants and global computer networks, owned by megacorporations, in cities where ordinary people barely get by. The heroes are hackers, mercenaries and other outsiders. The genre took shape in the early 1980s with the film Blade Runner and the novel Neuromancer.

What does cyberpunk mean?

Cyberpunk means stories where cutting-edge technology meets a broken society. The "cyber" half is computers, cybernetic implants and virtual worlds. The "punk" half is the people at the bottom who refuse the rules. StudioBinder sums it up as outsiders fighting oppressors, usually corporations, by technological means.

A typical cyberpunk story is set on Earth in the near future, not in a distant galaxy. Governments are weak, and real power sits with a few rich companies. Technology is used in ways its inventors never planned; Gibson's line "the street finds its own uses for things" is often quoted for this. The mood borrows heavily from film noir and hardboiled detective fiction: night, rain, moral gray areas, a tired hero.

Writer Lawrence Person described classic cyberpunk characters as alienated loners on the edge of society, living with rapid technological change, an ever-present web of computer data and "invasive modification of the human body." That last point matters. In cyberpunk the machine is often inside the person.

Where did the word cyberpunk come from?

The word comes from a short story. Bruce Bethke wrote "Cyberpunk" in 1980, and Amazing Stories published it in 1983. Bethke said he combined words for technology with words for troublemakers until one compound stuck.

The editor Gardner Dozois then used the word in his editorials and, in a 1984 Washington Post article, for a loose group of writers: Sterling, Gibson, Shiner, Cadigan and Bear. In 1986 Sterling edited Mirrorshades: The Cyberpunk Anthology, an attempt to define the movement from the inside.

The ideas are older than the name. The New Wave science fiction of the 1960s and 1970s, with writers such as Philip K. Dick, J. G. Ballard and Samuel R. Delany, had already turned away from bright utopias. Dick's 1968 novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? and John Brunner's 1975 novel The Shockwave Rider are often named as forerunners.

Which books and films define cyberpunk?

Two works from 1982 to 1984 defined the genre: the film Blade Runner gave it a look, and the novel Neuromancer gave it a vocabulary. Most later cyberpunk films, anime and games borrow from one or both.

Neuromancer (1984) was William Gibson's first novel. It follows Case, a hacker hired for a heist, and it set down terms the genre still uses, such as cyberspace and "jacking in." It is the only novel to win the Hugo, the Nebula and the Philip K. Dick Award. Blade Runner, directed by Ridley Scott, adapts Dick's novel and is set in a dystopian Los Angeles of 2019, where a former cop hunts escaped synthetic humans called replicants. It did poorly in theaters at first and later became a cult film; in 1993 the Library of Congress selected it for the National Film Registry.

What does cyberpunk look like on screen?

On screen, cyberpunk looks like a dense night city: rain, neon signs, giant video billboards, crowded street markets and huge corporate towers above them. Blade Runner set this template, and StudioBinder lists its ingredients as constant rain, overpopulation, electric billboards and humanoid androids.

Much of that look came from real places in East Asia. Ridley Scott said he imagined the Los Angeles of Blade Runner as "Hong Kong on a very bad day." Mamoru Oshii based the streets of Ghost in the Shell (1995) on Hong Kong too, because its chaotic streets mixed old and new. Kowloon Walled City, a famously overbuilt district of Hong Kong, is often cited as a model for cyberpunk cityscapes.

William Gibson has said that "Modern Japan simply was cyberpunk," and Neuromancer opens in Chiba, a Japanese industrial city. Critics have also pointed out a downside: Western cyberpunk often uses Asian cities and people as exotic scenery, a pattern called techno-Orientalism.

Cyberpunk cinema also borrows from older crime films. Blade Runner pays homage to classic film noir, with its shadows and cynical heroes. The editing term for a fast run of short shots is explained in what a montage is.

The look travels well beyond the genre. Empty, uncanny interiors from the internet's liminal space aesthetic and the pastel retro screens of vaporwave both borrow pieces of it. More film styles are collected in our film section.

How did Japan shape cyberpunk?

Japan gave cyberpunk its second home. Japanese cyberpunk began in 1982 with Katsuhiro Otomo's manga Akira, and Otomo's own 1988 anime film made it famous worldwide. Ghost in the Shell, Battle Angel Alita and Cowboy Bebop followed.

Japanese cyberpunk grew from different roots than the American kind. Wikipedia notes that when Akira began, cyberpunk novels had not yet been translated into Japanese; the Japanese strand came partly from the local punk music scene and the punk films of Sogo Ishii. Live-action titles such as Tetsuo: The Iron Man (1989) took body modification to extremes.

The influence ran back to Hollywood. The Wachowskis took several ideas for The Matrix (1999) from Ghost in the Shell, including the falling green code inspired by that film's opening credits. Is Cyberpunk 2077 the same as cyberpunk?

No. Cyberpunk is the genre; Cyberpunk 2077 is one game inside it. Many people searching for "cyberpunk" today mean the game or the anime, so the difference is worth spelling out.

Cyberpunk 2077 is a 2020 action role-playing game made by the Polish studio CD Projekt Red. It is set in Night City, a dystopian metropolis in California, and based on Mike Pondsmith's tabletop role-playing game Cyberpunk, first released in 1988. Keanu Reeves voices Johnny Silverhand, a rock star whose digital copy lives in the hero's head.

Cyberpunk: Edgerunners (2022) is an anime miniseries set in the same Night City, made with the Japanese studio Trigger and released on Netflix. It follows David Martinez, a poor student who joins a crew of cybernetically enhanced mercenaries, called edgerunners. Its success sent many viewers back to the game. Both works use the genre's classic parts: implants, corporate rule, hackers and a city that eats its young.

Three film terms worth knowing alongside the genre: the Dutch angle, a deliberately tilted camera frame; Foley, sound effects recorded after shooting; and the short film.

Where should you start with cyberpunk?

Start with the two works the genre grew from: watch Blade Runner (1982) and read Neuromancer (1984). Then move to Japan with Akira (1988) and Ghost in the Shell (1995), and finish with The Matrix (1999) to see how those ideas reached Hollywood.

  1. Blade Runner (1982), for the look and mood.
  2. Akira (1988), for the energy of Japanese cyberpunk.
  3. Ghost in the Shell (1995), for questions about body and mind.
  4. The Matrix (1999), for the mainstream version.
  5. Blade Runner 2049 (2017), to see the 1982 world revisited.
  6. Cyberpunk: Edgerunners (2022), if you want a short, modern series.

Watching in roughly this order shows how each work answers the one before it. Cyberpunk keeps changing because its "future" keeps arriving: the networks and implants of 1984 are partly today's phones and apps, and new stories have to imagine past them.

Questions people ask

What is cyberpunk in simple words?

It is science fiction about a near future where computers, implants and artificial intelligence are everywhere, but most people are poor and big companies hold the power. The heroes are usually outsiders such as hackers, mercenaries or detectives who live on the edge of that society.

Who invented the word cyberpunk?

The American writer Bruce Bethke. He used it as the title of a short story written in 1980 and published in Amazing Stories in 1983. Editor Gardner Dozois then picked the word up and used it for writers such as William Gibson and Bruce Sterling.

What was the first cyberpunk movie?

Blade Runner (1982), directed by Ridley Scott, is usually named as the first major cyberpunk film. Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927) is called proto-cyberpunk, an ancestor rather than a member of the genre.

Is Cyberpunk 2077 based on a book?

No. The 2020 game by CD Projekt Red is set in the world of Mike Pondsmith's tabletop role-playing game Cyberpunk, first released in 1988. The 2022 anime series Cyberpunk: Edgerunners is set in the same world.

What is the difference between cyberpunk and dystopia?

Dystopia is any imagined bad society. Cyberpunk is one kind of dystopia with a specific recipe: a near-future city, advanced computer and body technology, corporations stronger than governments, and outsiders who use technology against the system.
