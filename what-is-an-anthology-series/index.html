In short

  • An anthology series tells a different story with a different set of characters in each episode or each season.
  • Episode anthologies like The Twilight Zone (CBS, 1959–1964) start fresh every week; season anthologies like True Detective (HBO, from 2014) start fresh every season.
  • Something always stays the same: a host, a setting, a theme, a group of actors or a shared world.
  • The format began on radio; The Collier Hour ran on NBC's Blue Network from 1927 to 1932.
  • Since 2021 the Emmys have one category for limited and anthology series; The White Lotus won it with its first season in 2022.

An anthology series is a TV, radio or streaming show that tells a different story with a different set of characters in each episode or each season. The title, the tone and often a theme stay the same; the plot and the people start over. The Twilight Zone changed stories every week. True Detective, American Horror Story and The White Lotus change them every season.

What does anthology series mean?

Wikipedia defines an anthology series as a written, radio, television, film or video game series that presents a different story and a different set of characters in each episode, season, segment or short. No Film School gives almost the same definition and adds that the format lets writers try different genres, characters and situations under one clear title.

The word itself is old. It comes from the ancient Greek anthología, "flower-gathering". Meleager of Gadara used the idea around 60 BCE for a collection of short poems. An anthology show works the same way: a bunch of separate stories gathered under one name.

StudioBinder sums up what makes a show an anthology in three points. The entries share an identity (a genre, often the same creators or cast), each one works on its own, and nothing forces you to watch them in order.

Episode anthologies are the older model. The Twilight Zone ran for five seasons on CBS, from 2 October 1959 to 19 June 1964, and each episode was a standalone story, often with a surprise ending, a plot twist, and a moral. Its creator, Rod Serling, wrote or co-wrote 92 of the 156 episodes.

Season anthologies arrived later. Wikipedia says American Horror Story introduced the season-long model in the United States in 2011: each season is one complete story, and several actors come back in different roles. True Detective followed on HBO on 12 January 2014, with each season a self-contained story with a new cast. StudioBinder notes that the season model is the more common of the two today.

What stays the same from story to story?

The stories are separate, but something usually ties them together. What it is differs from series to series.

  1. A host. On the radio show Inner Sanctum Mystery the host who opened and closed each story was the only constant. Rod Serling did the same on The Twilight Zone, with monologues at the start and end of each episode. Alfred Hitchcock created, produced and hosted Alfred Hitchcock Presents, which aired on CBS and NBC between 1955 and 1965.
  2. A theme. Black Mirror keeps returning to technology and media, with most episodes set in a near-future dystopia.
  3. A place. The White Lotus follows guests and staff at a fictional luxury resort chain: Hawaii in season one, Sicily in season two, Thailand in season three.
  4. A company of actors. Four Star Playhouse, which began on CBS in 1952, rotated a small group of stars, including Dick Powell, Charles Boyer and David Niven, through a new drama each week. Inside No. 9 has new casts every time, but almost every episode stars its writers, Steve Pemberton and Reece Shearsmith.
  5. A shared world. Fargo's five seasons each have their own setting, cast and characters, yet they share continuity with the 1996 Coen brothers film and overlap in small ways.

Some links are tiny. Every episode of Inside No. 9 is set somewhere connected with the number nine, and a brass hare statue is hidden in each one.

Is Black Mirror an anthology? Common cases

People often search whether a particular show counts. Here are the best-known cases.

Black Mirror is an episode anthology. Charlie Brooker created it as a British series inspired by The Twilight Zone. It has 33 episodes in seven series plus a special, and the interactive film Bandersnatch (2018). The first two series aired on Channel 4 in 2011 and 2013; Netflix took over from 2016.

Beef is a season anthology. Season 1 put Steven Yeun and Ali Wong in a feud that starts with road rage. Season 2 moves to two new couples, played by Oscar Isaac, Carey Mulligan, Cailee Spaeny and Charles Melton. What returns is the feud, not the characters.

The White Lotus started out as something else. HBO ordered it in October 2020 as a miniseries and turned it into an anthology in August 2021. Only three actors have appeared in more than one season, among them Jennifer Coolidge as Tanya McQuoid in seasons one and two.

Anthology series vs limited series vs miniseries

A limited series tells one story and is not planned to return. Miniseries is the older name for the same idea; StudioBinder calls it an older synonym. An anthology tells one story, ends it, and then tells another under the same title.

The overlap is the season anthology. One season of True Detective is complete, just like a limited series, so awards bodies treat the two together. The Emmy category history shows it: Outstanding Limited Series from 2015, and Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series from 2021. The White Lotus won that award in 2022 with its first season. In 2024 the fifth season of Fargo and True Detective: Night Country were nominated, and in 2025 the seventh season of Black Mirror was.

The line can blur. StudioBinder points out that after The White Lotus's first season won in the limited category, its second and third seasons competed in the drama categories.

Where did anthology series start?

On the radio. Wikipedia names The Collier Hour, broadcast on NBC's Blue Network from 1927 to 1932, as one of the earliest anthology programs. It adapted stories from Collier's Weekly to sell more copies of the magazine. Horror, science fiction, mystery and suspense anthologies filled radio schedules for decades; Suspense ran from 1942 to 1962.

Television picked up the format in the 1950s. Live anthology dramas such as The Philco Television Playhouse and The United States Steel Hour were especially popular in that Golden Age of Television. American networks also ran summer anthologies made of unsold pilots, the trial episodes explained in our guide to what a pilot episode is.

Animated anthologies and anthology films

Wikipedia's list of anthology series has a long animated section. Love, Death & Robots, created by Tim Miller for Netflix, is an adult animated anthology.

Blur Studio produces it, but individual episodes come from animation studios in several countries, and each episode is connected to one or more of the three words in the title. Wikipedia classes it as a web series, the kind of show made for online release covered in our guide to what a web series is. Treehouse of Horror (since 1990) and Robot Chicken (since 2005) also appear on Wikipedia's list of adult animated anthologies.

Anthology film series are much rarer. Wikipedia gives the Halloween franchise as an example of one that tried: Halloween III: Season of the Witch was meant to start a run of unrelated horror films, but the plan was shelved after a poor reception.

Why watch an anthology series?

You can start almost anywhere. StudioBinder says the minimal continuity suits viewers who prefer shorter stories, while the shared title still brings back people who like the show's themes and style.

A season-ending cliffhanger makes little sense when the next season starts a new story.

The makers get something too. StudioBinder says that with no long commitment, the format attracts A-list actors, who are often wary of multi-season shows. The first season of True Detective starred Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson.

Questions people ask

What does anthology series mean in simple words?

It is a show where each episode or each season is its own story, with its own characters. You can usually start anywhere and watch in any order. What links the stories is the title and something that repeats, such as a theme, a host or a place.

Is Black Mirror an anthology series?

Yes. Black Mirror, created by Charlie Brooker, is a British anthology series. Each episode is a standalone story, most of them set in a near future with technology that goes wrong. The first two series aired on Channel 4 in 2011 and 2013, and later series moved to Netflix.

How is Beef an anthology series?

Each season of Beef has a new feud and new leads. Season 1 starred Steven Yeun and Ali Wong as two strangers whose road rage incident turns into a long feud. Season 2 follows two different couples, played by Oscar Isaac, Carey Mulligan, Cailee Spaeny and Charles Melton. The idea of a feud is what carries over.

How long is an anthology series?

There is no set length, because the show can keep adding stories. The original Twilight Zone made 156 episodes in five seasons. Inside No. 9 ran for 9 series and 55 half-hour episodes on BBC Two.

What is the difference between an anthology series and a limited series?

A limited series tells one story and ends. An anthology tells one story, then another, under the same title. Because each anthology season is complete, a single season can compete in the Emmy category for limited or anthology series.
