A manhwa is a Korean comic. In Korean, manhwa simply means comics, but outside Korea the word refers to comics made in South Korea. Most modern manhwa are webtoons: digital comics in full color, published as one long vertical strip you read by scrolling down on a phone. Manhwa reads left to right, like English, which is one quick way to tell it apart from Japanese manga.

What does manhwa mean?

Manhwa (만화) is the general Korean word for comics and cartoons. English speakers borrowed it to mean Korean comics specifically, the same way "manga" in English means Japanese comics rather than comics in general.

The word has a shared root with its neighbors. Wikipedia explains that manhwa, Japanese manga and Chinese manhua are cognates, all written with the same two characters, often translated as "impromptu sketches." CBR adds that the creators have matching titles too: a manga artist is a mangaka, a manhwa artist a manhwaga, and a manhua artist a manhuajia.

How is manhwa different from manga and manhua?

The short answer is country, reading direction and format. Manga is Japanese, manhwa is Korean, manhua is Chinese. Manga and manhua traditionally read right to left; manhwa reads left to right. Digital manhwa and manhua are usually in color, while manga is usually black and white.

Art style differs too, though less strictly. CBR notes that Osamu Tezuka's big eyes and exaggerated expressions shaped manga, while manhwa and manhua characters tend toward more realistic body proportions. Wikipedia adds that manhwa faces often stay stylized even when bodies are drawn realistically. On content, CBR points to Korean stories about beauty culture, such as True Beauty, and to Chinese wuxia and cultivation fantasy, while censorship of romance is usually strictest in manhua.

What is a webtoon?

A webtoon is a digital comic made for scrolling. Each episode is one long vertical strip instead of separate pages, it is usually in full color, and some include music or small animations. Webtoons started in South Korea, and the word joins "web" and "cartoon."

Wikipedia dates the term to 8 August 2000, when the Korean internet service Chollian first used it. The Korean portal Daum launched Daum Webtoon in 2003, and Naver launched Naver Webtoon in 2004. In 2014 Naver's global service, then called LINE Webtoon, brought translated Korean series to readers worldwide. Apps such as Lezhin and TappyToon also publish manhwa in English, CBR notes.

The format changes how a story feels. CBR points out that vertical scrolling lets artists show something falling or time passing in a way a printed page cannot: the reader's own thumb moves the camera. Episodes usually come out weekly. Where did manhwa come from?

Korean comics go back to the political cartoons and propaganda of the early and mid 20th century. The cartoonist Kim Yong-hwan started Korea's first comic magazine, Manhwa Haengjin, in 1948, though the authorities soon shut it down over its cover.

Comics boomed in the 1950s and 1960s. New genres appeared, including sunjeong, romance aimed at young women, and comic rooms called manhwabang let readers pay to sit and read. From the mid-1960s, government censorship and a distribution monopoly hit the industry hard, CBR and Wikipedia both note.

The internet changed everything. After South Korea's economic crisis at the end of the 1990s, much of manhwa moved online, and webtoons also helped creators get around strict print censorship. For years American publishers such as Tokyopop even sold manhwa to English readers under the manga label.

Which manhwa became anime and TV series?

Several manhwa have become anime, mostly made by Japanese studios, and live-action Korean series. The best known are Tower of God, The God of High School, Sweet Home, True Beauty and Solo Leveling.

  1. Tower of God, by S.I.U. Serialized on Naver Webtoon since June 2010, in English from July 2014. Its anime adaptation began on 1 April 2020.
  2. The God of High School. A Korean webtoon adapted into an anime series in 2020, as CBR notes.
  3. Sweet Home, by Kim Carnby and Hwang Young-chan. A Naver webtoon turned into a Korean horror series; the first season came out on Netflix on 18 December 2020.
  4. True Beauty. A romance manhwa about beauty culture that was adapted into a TV series, according to CBR.
  5. Solo Leveling. A web novel by Chugong (2016), made into a webtoon drawn by Jang Sung-rak, known as Dubu, from 4 March 2018. A-1 Pictures adapted it as an anime that aired from January to March 2024, with a second season from January to March 2025.

These shows have blurred the line between anime and its sources. Viewers who meet Solo Leveling as an anime may be surprised that the story behind it is Korean, not Japanese. CBR also compares Solo Leveling's power fantasy to the Japanese isekai genre.

How do you read a manhwa?

Read a webtoon manhwa from top to bottom by scrolling, one episode per strip. Printed manhwa reads like an English book, from left to right and top to bottom, page by page.

  1. Pick a legal app or site. CBR mentions Webtoon, Lezhin and TappyToon as places to read manhwa and manhua officially.
  2. Scroll slowly. Panels are spaced for timing; a long gap between two panels often stands for a pause or a fall.
  3. Expect weekly episodes. Most webtoons publish one chapter a week and run in seasons with breaks.
  4. Check the original. If you came from an anime, the webtoon often continues past the last episode.

Questions people ask

What is a manhwa in simple words?

A manhwa is a comic from South Korea. In Korean the word just means comics of any kind, but English speakers use it for Korean comics in particular, especially the color webtoons people read by scrolling on a phone.

What is the difference between manhwa and manga?

Manga is Japanese, manhwa is Korean. Manga is usually printed in black and white and read right to left. Manhwa reads left to right, and modern manhwa are mostly digital webtoons in full color, built as one long vertical strip instead of pages.

What is the difference between manhwa and manhua?

Manhwa comes from Korea, manhua from China, Taiwan and Hong Kong. Both are often published digitally in color, but CBR notes that manhua is traditionally read right to left and usually faces stricter censorship, especially of romance.

Is Solo Leveling a manhwa?

Yes, in its best-known form. Solo Leveling began as a Korean web novel by Chugong in 2016. Its webtoon version, drawn by Dubu, started on KakaoPage in 2018, and the anime by the Japanese studio A-1 Pictures adapted that story in 2024.

Is a webtoon the same as a manhwa?

Not exactly. A webtoon is a format: a digital comic published as a long vertical strip, usually in color. Manhwa is Korean comics in general. Most new manhwa are webtoons, but print manhwa exist, and webtoons are now made in other countries too.
