In short

  • Baroque is a European style in painting, sculpture, architecture and music from the early 1600s to the 1750s, which began in Rome.
  • Its works are dramatic and emotional, with strong contrasts of light and shadow, diagonal compositions and illusionistic effects.
  • The Catholic Church's Council of Trent (1545–1563) wanted art that engaged believers directly and emotionally.
  • The name was first an insult and may come from the Portuguese barroco, an irregular pearl.
  • Key artists include Caravaggio, Bernini, Rubens, Artemisia Gentileschi, Velázquez, Rembrandt and Vermeer.

Baroque art is the dramatic style that flourished in European painting, sculpture and architecture from the early 1600s to the 1750s. It began in Rome and spread across Catholic and Protestant countries alike. A Baroque work wants a reaction: it shows the most intense moment of a story, sets strong light against deep shadow, and pulls the eye along diagonals. Caravaggio, Bernini, Rubens, Rembrandt and Velázquez are the best-known names.

What is Baroque art in simple words?

It is art built to move you. The National Gallery in London defines Baroque painting and sculpture as large, usually 17th-century works "of a dramatic and exuberant nature" that use diagonal compositions and illusionistic effects to impress the viewer. It names Rubens and Bernini as the artists most often described as Baroque in this sense.

Wikipedia dates the wider Baroque style from the early 1600s to the 1750s. It covered architecture, music, dance, painting, sculpture and poetry, followed the Renaissance and Mannerism, and was followed by Rococo and Neoclassicism.

So "Baroque" names both a period and a manner. A painting from 1650 is from the Baroque period; a painting that looks theatrical, emotional and lit like a stage is Baroque in style.

Where does the word baroque come from?

The word started as an insult. The National Gallery says it was originally derogatory and may come from the Portuguese barroco, an irregular pearl. Wikipedia quotes a French dictionary of 1694 that used "baroque" only for pearls that are imperfectly round, and notes another proposed source, a medieval Latin term from logic.

By the mid-19th century critics were using the word to ridicule post-Renaissance art. In 1855 the art historian Jacob Burckhardt wrote that Baroque artists "despised and abused detail." In 1888 Heinrich Wölfflin published Renaissance und Barock, which Wikipedia calls the first serious academic work on the style.

Why did Baroque art begin? The Counter-Reformation

The Baroque grew out of a religious conflict. In response to the Protestant Reformation, the Catholic Church held the Council of Trent from 1545 to 1563. Wikipedia says the council wanted the arts to engage viewers emotionally with religious themes.

The council's decrees said little directly about painting, but later Catholic writers argued that church paintings should show their subjects clearly and powerfully, without the affected elegance of Mannerism. Many art historians see that demand behind the innovations of Caravaggio and the Carracci brothers, who were all working in Rome around 1600.

The style did not stay Catholic. Wikipedia points to important Baroque art in Protestant and non-absolutist states too. In France the big royal project was the expansion of Versailles, begun in 1661. French Baroque is more restrained than the Italian kind and is also called the Louis XIV style.

How to recognise a Baroque painting or sculpture

Many Baroque works share a few habits. Check a picture against this list:

  1. The moment is the most dramatic one: the blow, the conversion, the turn of the head, not the calm before or after.
  2. Light is strong and selective, picking out faces and hands against deep shadow. This contrast is called chiaroscuro.
  3. The composition runs on diagonals.
  4. Colour is rich and deep.
  5. Figures are caught in the middle of an action.

Wikipedia's comparison makes the difference from the Renaissance easy to see. Michelangelo's David stands composed and still. Bernini's David is caught in the act of hurling the stone. The Renaissance prized calm rationality; the Baroque wanted emotion and passion.

Caravaggio and the light that made the style

Wikipedia says Caravaggio's realistic approach shocked his contemporaries and opened a new chapter in the history of painting. He painted figures from life and spotlit them against dark backgrounds.

The Calling of Saint Matthew (1599–1600) shows how. It was painted for the Contarelli Chapel of San Luigi dei Francesi in Rome, where it still hangs. Matthew, a tax collector, sits at a table with four other men. Christ and Saint Peter have entered the room, and Christ points at Matthew. A beam of light illuminates the faces of the men at the table. The canvas measures 322 × 340 cm. A passage quoted by Wikipedia credits its "clear legibility," so different from many Mannerist paintings, for the work's enormous popularity. Wikipedia counts Caravaggio among the greatest painters of the period, with Velázquez, Rembrandt, Rubens, Poussin and Vermeer.

Artemisia Gentileschi's Judith Slaying Holofernes (1614–20) is in the Uffizi in Florence. For the lighting technique itself, see what chiaroscuro is.

Bernini and Baroque Rome

How Baroque looked in different countries

The style changed as it travelled. Mediterranean countries, Wikipedia says, generally took up the full Baroque approach, while Dutch painting grew out of northern realist traditions.

Region What Baroque looked like there Example
Italy Led Baroque painting; Caravaggio and the Carracci worked in Rome around 1600 Caravaggio, The Calling of Saint Matthew (1599–1600)
Flanders More openly Baroque than the Dutch, keeping the traditional categories of painting Rubens, Judgement of Paris (c. 1636), National Gallery, London
Dutch Republic Grew from northern realist traditions: very little religious art, little history painting, much still life, everyday scenes and landscape Rembrandt, The Night Watch (1642); Vermeer, The Art of Painting (1666–67)
Spain Velázquez is counted among the greatest painters of the period Velázquez, Las Meninas (1656–57), Prado
France Shaped by classicism; Poussin and Claude Lorrain remained based in Rome Poussin, The Rape of the Sabine Women (1637–38), Louvre

Wikipedia calls The Night Watch a classic example of Baroque art. It is a group portrait of a civic militia company, 379.5 by 453.5 cm, which the Rijksmuseum calls Rembrandt's largest and most famous canvas. The museum says Rembrandt was the first to paint the figures in a group portrait actually doing something: the captain is telling his lieutenant to start the company marching. Light picks out the captain's gesturing hand and a young girl in the background, the company mascot.

When did the Baroque end?

Gradually, in the 18th century. Wikipedia says that by the 1730s the Baroque had evolved into the lighter, more flamboyant rocaille, or Rococo, which lasted in France and Central Europe until the mid-to-late 18th century. Neoclassicism came after it.

Baroque is the step between the Renaissance and Rococo; for what came before, see what Renaissance art is. The other big movements, from Art Deco to abstract art, are compared in our guide to art styles, and more techniques, such as gouache, are collected under painting and graphics.

Questions people ask

What is Baroque art in simple words?

It is the big, dramatic art of seventeenth-century Europe. Paintings show the most intense moment of a story, lit like a stage, with deep shadows around the action. Sculptures twist and move. Churches and palaces are designed to overwhelm you as you walk in.

What are the main characteristics of Baroque art?

Drama, movement and emotion; rich, deep colour; strong contrasts of light and dark, called chiaroscuro; diagonal compositions; and illusionistic effects meant to impress the viewer. The National Gallery in London sums it up as large, dramatic and exuberant works that use these devices to impress.

Why is it called Baroque?

The word was originally an insult. The National Gallery says it may come from the Portuguese barroco, meaning an irregular pearl, and a French dictionary of 1694 still used it only for pearls that are imperfectly round. By the mid-nineteenth century critics used it to ridicule post-Renaissance art.

What is the difference between Renaissance and Baroque art?

Renaissance art prized calm rationality; Baroque art aimed for emotion and passion. Wikipedia's example is David: Michelangelo shows him composed and still before the fight, while Bernini's sculpture catches him in the act of hurling the stone.

Who are the most famous Baroque artists?

In Italy, Caravaggio, Artemisia Gentileschi and the sculptor and architect Gian Lorenzo Bernini; in Flanders, Peter Paul Rubens; in Spain, Diego Velázquez; in the Dutch Republic, Rembrandt and Johannes Vermeer; in France, Nicolas Poussin.
