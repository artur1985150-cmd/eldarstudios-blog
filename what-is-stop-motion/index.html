In short

  • Stop motion is animation made by moving a real object in tiny steps and photographing it after each step; played back, the object seems to move by itself.
  • Puppets with movable joints and clay figures are the most common subjects, but cutouts, toys and even people (pixilation) can be animated.
  • Landmarks include King Kong (1933), Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (1964), The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993), Chicken Run (2000) and Coraline (2009).
  • Aardman in Bristol and Laika in Portland, Oregon are the best-known stop-motion studios working today.
  • Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio (2022) won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Stop motion is an animation technique in which a real object is moved a tiny amount, photographed, moved again and photographed again, hundreds or thousands of times. When the photos are played in sequence, the object seems to move on its own. Puppets with jointed skeletons and clay figures are the usual subjects, but paper, toys, sand and even people can be animated this way. Famous examples include King Kong, The Nightmare Before Christmas, Wallace & Gromit and Coraline.

How does stop-motion animation work?

An animator poses a figure, takes one photograph, shifts the figure slightly and takes the next. Film shows 24 frames each second. Played back at that speed, the small changes between photos merge into movement. Nothing on set actually moves while the camera records.

The arithmetic explains why it is slow. Many productions shoot "on twos", holding each pose for two frames, which still means 12 changes for every second of screen time. By the count in Wikipedia's claymation article, a 30-minute film made that way needs about 21,600 separate changes, and a 90-minute feature about 64,800.

Everything else on set must stay still. Lights, camera and background have to be the same from one frame to the next, or the picture flickers. Animators call a set that is in the middle of a shot a "hot set": nobody touches it, and scenes are often finished within a day, because humidity can soften clay and shift the set overnight.

What are the main kinds of stop motion?

Stop motion is a family. What changes is the material in front of the camera: jointed puppets, clay, flat cutouts, live people or everyday objects. Each has its own name and its own look, and many films combine two or three.

Kind What is moved Example
Puppet animation Puppets with movable joints, often on a metal armature The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993), Coraline (2009)
Claymation Figures of clay or plasticine Wallace & Gromit, Gumby, Pingu
Cutout animation Flat pieces of paper, cloth or photographs Terry Gilliam's sequences for Monty Python's Flying Circus
Pixilation Live actors, photographed pose by pose Many music videos and art shorts
Object animation Toys, action figures, food, household things Robot Chicken (since 2005), with action figures
Model animation Lifelike models built around an armature, mixed with live action King Kong (1933), Jason and the Argonauts (1963)

How can you tell a film is stop motion?

Look for real textures and small imperfections. Fabric has visible weave and clay keeps the animators' fingerprints. Light comes from real lamps, so shadows are sharp and physical. Fast moves can look a little jumpy because a still puppet has no motion blur.

Adobe describes this as the "inherent imperfection and subtle flicker" that gives stop motion a handmade feel. Some studios now smooth these signs away. Laika received a Scientific and Technology Oscar for pioneering rapid prototyping, or 3D printing, for character animation in stop motion. The studio says it brought leaps in facial animation, motion blur and effects, so a modern Laika film can look smoother than older puppet work.

CGI can also copy the look. Aardman's Flushed Away was made by computer to imitate claymation. If a "clay" character bends in ways no material could, or the surface is too perfect, it is probably digital.

Who invented stop motion?

No single person did. Several early filmmakers used the trick around 1900 to 1910. J. Stuart Blackton's The Haunted Hotel (1907) made it popular, and Blackton and Albert E. Smith's The Humpty Dumpty Circus (1908, now lost) animated wooden toys. Smith later called it the first stop-motion picture in America.

Ladislas Starevich turned the trick into an art. Working in Kaunas around 1909 and 1910, he wanted to film fighting stag beetles, but the live insects would not cooperate under hot lamps. He wired the legs of dried beetles and animated them instead. In Moscow he went on to make insect films with real stories, such as The Cameraman's Revenge.

In the United States, Willis O'Brien made his first stop-motion film, The Dinosaur and the Missing Link, in 1915. Starevich, by then in Paris, finished the first stop-motion feature, The Tale of the Fox, in 1930.

What are the most famous stop-motion films?

The best-known stop-motion films span almost a century, from a giant ape to a wooden boy. The list below is in date order and names who made each film. Where a film won a major award, the award is noted.

  1. King Kong (1933): Willis O'Brien's animation of the ape is widely seen as a milestone.
  2. Jason and the Argonauts (1963): one of Ray Harryhausen's fantasy films, made after he learned under O'Brien.
  3. Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (1964): the Rankin/Bass puppet TV special, broadcast every year since 1964.
  4. Closed Mondays (1974): Will Vinton and Bob Gardiner's clay short, the first stop-motion film to win an Oscar.
  5. The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993): directed by Henry Selick and produced by Tim Burton; the highest-grossing stop-motion film of its time.
  6. Chicken Run (2000): Aardman's feature, which became the highest-grossing stop-motion animated film in history.
  7. Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit (2005): Nick Park won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.
  8. Coraline (2009): Laika's first feature, directed by Henry Selick and nominated for an Academy Award.
  9. Fantastic Mr. Fox (2009), Anomalisa (2015) and Isle of Dogs (2018): three more of the notable stop-motion features made since 2000.
  10. Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio (2022): the first animated film from a streaming service to win the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Which studios make stop motion today?

Two studios stand out. Aardman in Bristol, started in 1972 by Peter Lord and David Sproxton, made Morph, Wallace & Gromit, Chicken Run and Shaun the Sheep. Laika in Portland, Oregon, founded in 2005, has made Coraline, ParaNorman, The Boxtrolls, Kubo and the Two Strings, Missing Link and Wildwood.

Laika's list of firsts is long. By its own account, Coraline was the first stop-motion film conceived and photographed in stereoscopic 3D. ParaNorman was its first film to use color 3D printers. Kubo and the Two Strings won the BAFTA for Best Animated Film, and Missing Link won the 2020 Golden Globe for Best Animated Feature Film. Wikipedia describes Laika as the stop-motion successor to Will Vinton Studios, the Portland company of Will Vinton, who coined the word "claymation".

Stop motion is also strong on television, especially for children. Pingu, the Swiss series, ran to over 150 five-minute episodes between 1986 and 2000. Bob the Builder first aired on the BBC in November 1998.

Why does stop motion look so different from CGI?

Because everything in the frame is real. Directors who choose it, among them Guillermo del Toro, Henry Selick, Tim Burton and Travis Knight, point to real textures and real light. CGI surfaces, by comparison, can look artificial. The slight unevenness is the point.

Del Toro put it most directly. For Pinocchio he wanted "the expressiveness and the material nature of a handmade piece of animation", which he described as an exercise in carving, painting and sculpting. That handmade quality is what a viewer notices first, even without knowing the technique: the characters look like things you could pick up.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between stop motion and claymation?

Claymation is one kind of stop motion. Stop motion covers any object moved between frames: puppets, toys, paper cutouts, sand or people. Claymation uses figures made of clay or plasticine. Wallace & Gromit is claymation; The Nightmare Before Christmas uses puppets, so it is stop motion but not claymation.

How many frames per second is stop motion?

Film runs at 24 frames per second. Many stop-motion productions shoot "on twos", holding each pose for two frames, so they need 12 changes for every second of film. Adobe suggests at least 12 distinct frames per second for smooth motion.

Is stop motion the same as time-lapse?

No. Time-lapse films real events slowly and speeds them up, like a flower opening. Stop motion builds movement that never happened: an animator moves the object between each photo, so the motion exists only on film.

What was the first stop-motion feature film?

Ladislas Starevich finished The Tale of the Fox (Le Roman de Renard) in Paris in 1930, the first stop-motion feature. Soundtrack problems delayed its release; it came out with a German soundtrack in 1937 and a French one in 1941.

Why do studios still make stop motion when CGI exists?

Directors like its look. Real materials have real texture, and light falls on them as it does in life. Guillermo del Toro said he wanted the expressiveness and material nature of handmade animation for Pinocchio. Henry Selick, Tim Burton and Travis Knight have made similar choices.
